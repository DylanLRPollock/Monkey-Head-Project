## Introduction — How a Collective Speaks Without Becoming One Unrestricted Voice

Huey is intended to become one coherent, embodied system composed of many distinguishable participants. Its pebbles have individual configurations, bounded responsibilities, and private state. Its districts have local work and political standing. Its Senate, President, and Supreme Court perform different forms of constitutional work. Its collective Pulse periodically examines authorized shared information and proposes coordinated work. Its physical body must continue to protect itself even when the deliberative machinery is delayed or unavailable.

Such a system cannot preserve these differences if its participants communicate by freely reading one common, unrestricted transcript. Nor can it remain accountable if a message may be forged, delivered to the wrong office, silently rewritten, or interpreted as authority merely because it arrived at a powerful process.

**HIMS — Huey Internal Messaging System — is the proposed communications and message-trust architecture intended to make those boundaries real.** It gives a name and a future technical home to messages that originate somewhere, travel under a particular authority, reach an intended recipient, and leave the appropriate record. HIMS is more than an inbox. It is the infrastructure that would allow the collective to communicate without pretending that communication, permission, interpretation, and action are the same operation.

The older HIMS research already contained several useful ideas: layered interfaces, validation, routing, delivery, the Scrolls, a Keeper of the Scrolls, six provisional trust classes, separate sender and device credentials, end-to-end encryption for protected material, sealed citizen storage, quarantine, acknowledgements, and a possible mail-style component called ThunderMail. Those ideas remain relevant. The newer architecture, however, changes their setting. The Rock is now the durable shared-information and governance-record store, not the metaphorical pooled mind. Four districts with 64 founding citizens each are proposed, not the earlier 128-citizen total. The legislative branch is provisionally called the Senate, not Parliament. The collective Pulse is a coordinating and analytical process, not a new sovereign office.

This chapter therefore asks a practical question: **can Huey move information among independently bounded actors without allowing the communications system to become an invisible ruler, a universal surveillance mechanism, or a single point of collapse?**

The answer is not supplied by an architectural drawing. It must eventually be established through access controls, cryptographic keys, bounded services, reproducible tests, and evidence of what happens when a message is invalid, a key is compromised, or a district goes offline. HIMS should be designed to support Huey's constitutional experiment; it cannot make that experiment legitimate by itself.

## I. The Problem HIMS Is Meant to Solve

At the simplest level, two programs can exchange information through a file, a socket, an API call, or a queue. That alone is not sufficient for Huey. Within a governed collective, the same bytes may have radically different consequences depending on who sent them, where they came from, what they claim to be, and what the recipient is lawfully allowed to do.

Consider four superficially similar messages:

- A pebble reports that a cooling sensor appears anomalous.
- A district requests additional time to finish an investigation.
- The President directs implementation of an already authorized maintenance task.
- The Supreme Court records a constitutional ruling.

All four can be represented as text. Their meaning and authority, however, must not be determined merely by the fluency of the text or by the model's willingness to follow it. A report is evidence, not an order. A request is not approval. An executive directive must be checked against its origin and delegated scope. A ruling must be attributable to the correct office and process.

The challenge becomes greater when citizens use the same replaceable inference resource—the Walnut. If one model instance is asked to impersonate a sender and then also validate that sender's message, the claimed institutional separation becomes little more than role-play. Independent processes, independently checked permissions, separate storage domains, and bounded credentials are therefore central to the design. Different prompts alone do not produce trustworthy separation.

HIMS must address several related problems:

1. **Authenticity:** did an identified actor actually issue the message?
2. **Authorization:** was that actor permitted to send this type of message to this destination, and may the recipient perform the requested operation?
3. **Confidentiality:** which parties may read the content, and which may see only routing data?
4. **Integrity:** can changes, omissions, or substitutions be detected?
5. **Delivery:** was the message accepted, queued, delivered, read, or acted upon—and what evidence supports each claim?
6. **Continuity:** which parts of the exchange must remain recoverable as private, restricted, official, or historical record?
7. **Failure containment:** what occurs when the route is unavailable, credentials are invalid, the message is malicious, or a district has ceased responding?

These are security and reliability questions as well as governance questions. HIMS can provide the technical evidence and boundaries through which legitimate institutions operate. It cannot independently determine which institution ought to win a constitutional dispute.

## II. Distinguishing Transport, Trust, Authority, and Memory

The most important architectural distinction is that four functions must remain separately intelligible.

**Transport** moves information from a sender to an intended destination. A transport layer can acknowledge that bytes arrived without judging whether their contents are true or binding.

**Trust verification** checks the identity of the sender, the integrity of an envelope or payload, the validity of its credentials, and whether the proposed route satisfies published rules. Valid authentication does not guarantee truthful content. A legitimate citizen can still be mistaken; an authorized office can still misuse an allowed channel.

**Authority** determines whether a particular act is permitted. A message requesting an actuator movement, publication of a law, alteration of a citizen registry, or protected hibernation needs separate authorization from the relevant policy or constitutional process. A signed message should never become self-authorizing simply because its signature verifies.

**Memory** preserves what must outlast the delivery event. A message may have an ephemeral delivery record, a sealed private body, an official procedural receipt, or a committed entry in the Rock. Not every transmission needs to become public memory, and not every acknowledgement becomes an act of government.

The proposed sequence is therefore closer to:

**Identify → authenticate → classify → validate route → deliver → independently authorize any requested action → commit the resulting record as appropriate.**

Some operations may require authorization before delivery as well, especially when delivery itself would disclose protected content. The sequence is a conceptual aid, not a universal ordering guarantee.

These distinctions prevent several damaging shortcuts. The Keeper of the Scrolls may see that a court message was signed and delivered without being allowed to interpret the case. A district may receive a presidential tasking without giving the President unrestricted access to its private records. The Rock may accept an officially authorized outcome without allowing every citizen to write arbitrary content directly into shared history. A physical interlock may protect an overheating machine without waiting for HIMS, a model, or a Senate vote.

The design principle is **a message can carry a claim to authority, but only the receiving boundary and the lawful authority system can validate that claim for the requested action**.

## III. What HIMS Is — and What It Is Not

HIMS is best understood as a collection of narrow services governed by shared communication rules. A single daemon might provide several services during early experimentation, but combining deployment processes should not erase their logical separation or grant every subsystem the same privileges.

The intended scope includes addressing, identity verification, key references, message envelopes, content protection, delivery queues, authorization referrals, provenance metadata, receipts, rejection records, and continuity-aware integration with storage. A mature implementation may also require subscription channels, broadcast controls, synchronization across districts, version negotiation, emergency-notification interfaces, and offline recovery tools.

HIMS is **not**:

- Huey itself or the source of its identity;
- the Founding Father, Senate, President, or Supreme Court;
- a fourth governing branch;
- the Walnut or the collective Pulse;
- the Rock or the contents of sealed citizen vaults;
- an unlimited credential to execute all messages it delivers;
- a replacement for deterministic physical safety interlocks;
- proof that encrypted traffic is safe, truthful, or lawfully authorized.

A practical early prototype might use locally available operating-system permissions, carefully separated processes, standard cryptographic libraries, and a durable queue. That would be a test implementation of particular HIMS properties, not automatic completion of HIMS as a whole. The meaning of the HIMS name rests on demonstrable behaviour, not on which framework or product is chosen.

The governing intent is **local-first communications across genuine boundaries**, not a requirement to invent a novel network protocol or encryption algorithm where established, reviewed technology can be used.

## IV. Actors, Domains, and Communication Boundaries

The first design task is to identify the participants and their different kinds of access.

**Citizens / Pebbles.** Each active citizen has a unique active identifier, a private sealed-vault domain, and permissions related to its operational responsibilities and civic status. The citizen can submit work, opinions, reports, proposals, and voting traffic through the channels its role permits. Its personal memory does not become everyone else's memory merely because they share model weights or hardware.

**Districts.** A district coordinates local work, ballots, and resources. During the proposed diversified Pulse, each of four initial districts is provisionally mapped to one accelerator. Its administrative services may relay or batch traffic, but cannot impersonate individual citizens or manufacture a missing district vote.

**The Senate.** Legislative submissions, sponsored motions, district returns, ordinary laws, override proposals, and recorded outcomes move through official channels. HIMS should distinguish a citizen's suggestion from an authenticated legislative result, and a draft motion from an enacted decision.

**The Presidency.** The President can submit initiatives, issue bounded implementation directives under proper authority, provide official notifications, and receive reports. The proposed special priority route must remain distinguishable from an ordinary submission. A Vice President or successor requires its own authenticated office status; it does not inherit the prior officeholder's private key.

**The Supreme Court.** Case referrals, evidence references, deliberation drafts, signed rulings, recusals, and implementation-reconsideration requests may require separate access classes. A Court ruling must be attributable to a valid judicial process, not simply to a message addressed to the Court.

**The Founding Father.** Before ratification it requires controlled initialization capabilities. After lawful retirement, an advisory referral should carry only authorized evidence and a narrowly scoped response path. Tier Two protected-hibernation authority, if ultimately enacted, needs a separately constrained route and independent safety controls. The founder must not retain invisible routine administrator credentials.

**Collective Pulse.** The unified analytical phase may receive authorized shared information, identify anomalies, route work proposals, and refer problems to proper authorities. It is not entitled to inspect every private vault or silently carry out a recommendation as law.

**Keeper of the Scrolls / messaging services.** The historical Keeper name captures the stewardship of attributable routing and durable communication history. Whether a separate elected, assigned, or automated Keeper office remains in the final Constitution is unsettled. Validation and logging responsibilities must be technically enforced even if the office name changes.

**The Rock.** The shared-information and governance-record store receives authenticated references, committed decisions, and preserved history through authorized write services. Citizens may receive permitted shared reads. A HIMS relay is not thereby an administrator of the Rock.

**Human Counterpart and outside interfaces.** The human remains outside the ordinary internal branches and interacts through deliberately bounded development, diagnostic, conversational, and protected maintenance channels. External software, including PyGPT/PyGPT-net during development and a possible PyHuey integration, must not gain broad internal privileges merely because it provides a convenient user interface.

**Physical safety controllers and body services.** Sensor reports and control status can be forwarded into HIMS, but the machine's genuinely time-critical interlocks must be able to act without an LLM, network round trip, collective vote, or successful message delivery.

A permissions model is not meaningful until every actor's ability to send, read, execute, retain, delegate, and revoke is made explicit. The categories above are the candidate subjects of that model; their final capabilities belong to machine-facing policy and lawful authorization, not to conversational inference.

## V. A Layered HIMS Architecture

The original paper proposed four layers: interface, validation/routing, delivery, and Scrolls. That remains a useful foundation. The revision distinguishes several responsibilities inside those layers so that no service becomes an invisible sovereign.

**1. Origin and interface boundary.** An internal citizen, branch process, device adapter, or external gateway constructs a message from its own context. The originating actor should be identifiable separately from the operating-system process that carried its request. Untrusted text received from a web page or external user must retain that source label even after it is summarized by a local model.

**2. Identity and key-verification boundary.** Credentials are checked against an active identity registry, their intended use, revocation state, and the relevant trust domain. The verifier should check cryptographic proof rather than ask an LLM whether a purported identity *sounds* plausible.

**3. Classification and route-policy boundary.** The message is assigned or checked against a declared type, confidentiality class, destination, permitted route, and possible authority context. Sender-supplied labels are claims subject to validation, not final determinations of permission.

**4. Delivery boundary.** A bounded carrier queues, forwards, acknowledges, retries, or rejects messages as appropriate. Reliable carriage may be called ThunderMail if that name is retained, but the mail system remains a subordinate part of HIMS.

**5. Receipt and continuity boundary.** The Scrolls or event-ledger layer preserves sufficient evidence that an exchange occurred and what status it reached. Full encrypted payloads, minimal metadata, official records, and private content may be stored in different locations with different retention rules.

**6. Action authorization boundary.** A separate, properly scoped service or institution decides whether the requested downstream change may be executed. This boundary is not a replacement for the original four layers; it is an explicit reminder that message validation and delivery alone confer no executive authority.

The physical implementation may consolidate or separate these services for resource reasons. The security question is whether a compromise of one process can bypass every other boundary. A single privileged service that can decrypt all vaults, alter every ledger entry, impersonate every citizen, and control all actuators would negate the reasoning that led to HIMS in the first place.

## VI. Addressing and the Registry of Participants

A persistent messaging system needs more than readable names. Names can change; credentials rotate; citizens bifurcate; offices change hands. An address must refer to a clearly identified destination at a known time.

The research therefore distinguishes a **citizen identifier** from a **display name**, a **lineage reference**, a **district membership record**, an **office assignment**, and an **active cryptographic identity**. These may be linked but should not be made identical.

For example, one of the original 64 founding lineages might be informally labelled `F-017`. After two exact bifurcation generations, four active descendants may reference that ancestor in their displayed ancestry. The specific naming convention is unresolved. Their authenticated identities, however, must be unique even if all begin with a similar prompt and a shared historical snapshot. The retired ancestor must not still be able to send messages as an active citizen.

An office mailbox is similarly different from its current occupant. A valid message addressed to the President should be delivered according to a current, verifiable office assignment rather than to whichever citizen last occupied the office. A succession event changes the office's recipient and authorization context without silently handing the successor the predecessor's private vault or long-lived personal credentials.

The participant registry must eventually support identity activation, suspension, retirement, office elevation, recusal, replacement, bifurcation, credential rotation, and administrative recovery. Those events should be versioned and traceable. A stale cached address must not resurrect a retired identity after a fork or election.

The naming scheme can be human-readable. The authoritative registry must be machine-checkable.

## VII. Message Types, Trust Classes, and Authority Claims

The old HIMS research proposed six classes numbered zero through five. The taxonomy remains useful, provided we do not treat a single integer as a magical substitute for access policy. Message **type**, **confidentiality**, **urgency**, **durability**, and **authority** are different properties. An emergency thermal alert may be urgent without being confidential. A pebble's private note may be confidential without being urgent. A court judgment may be durable and authoritative without containing safety telemetry.

The inherited six-class scheme can serve as a provisional primary classification:

| Class | Working name | Illustrative material | Default treatment to research |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Ephemeral | Heartbeats, acknowledgements, transient scheduling notices | Small payloads; limited retention unless used as evidence of failure |
| 1 | Routine Internal | Ordinary task requests, status reports, non-sensitive coordination | Authenticated bounded delivery and appropriate logging |
| 2 | Pebble-Personal | Private drafts, sealed-vault messages, individual working memory | Restricted recipient set and protected content; minimal necessary route metadata |
| 3 | Office / Branch Restricted | Judicial deliberation drafts, protected office planning, non-public district matters | Role-specific read permissions and stricter access records |
| 4 | Constitutional / Official | Votes, validated legislative results, rulings, office succession, ratification, authorized directives | Strong provenance, tamper-evident durable recording, explicit authority checks |
| 5 | Safety / Preservation Critical | Cooling faults, power warnings, imminent preservation notices | Priority transport and audit; never a substitute for physical interlocks |

These categories are inherited research conventions, not proof of an implemented mandatory standard. Class numbers should not imply that Class 5 is automatically more secret than Class 4 or that Class 4 can override Class 3 confidentiality. A later schema could use several independent fields instead of forcing every concern into a linear sequence.

The following **message types** are also useful and should not be confused with trust classes: `NOTE`, `REQUEST`, `REPORT`, `TASK`, `DIRECTIVE`, `MOTION`, `BALLOT`, `BALLOT_RETURN`, `RULING`, `ALERT`, `PRESERVATION_NOTICE`, `RECEIPT`, `REFUSAL`, `REVOCATION`, and `EXTERNAL_INPUT`. These names are examples, not an adopted wire format.

A `DIRECTIVE` message containing the words “Supreme Court” does not make it judicial. A `BALLOT_RETURN` whose author claims to be an entire district cannot be counted merely because it was routed from a district machine. A `PRESERVATION_NOTICE` describing thermal danger should be checked against its trustworthy source, but the immediate protective controller must remain able to respond even if the message is missing.

This leads to an important research improvement over the old document: **the trust class describes handling requirements; it does not assign power.** Power comes from the valid authority path in force at the moment the relevant action is requested.

## VIII. A Candidate Message Envelope

A common envelope would allow independently developed services to exchange messages while preserving their status. The final serialization format, transport protocol, cryptographic suite, and schema version are unresolved. The following fields describe a candidate logical contract, not a production implementation or a settled public API.

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `message_id` | Unique identifier supporting deduplication and audit |
| `schema_version` | Declares how the envelope is to be interpreted |
| `sender_id` / `sender_role` | Names the originating identity and any claimed active office |
| `recipient_id` / `recipient_role` | Identifies destination or lawful subscription group |
| `district_context` | Records applicable district or collective jurisdiction when relevant |
| `message_type` / `handling_class` | Distinguishes the operation from the required protection |
| `created_at` / `expires_at` | Enables time bounds and stale-message rejection |
| `sequence_or_nonce` | Supports replay detection in an appropriate scope |
| `conversation_or_case_id` | Relates a message to prior lawful discussion or proceeding |
| `parent_event_id` | Associates a reply, appeal, or revision with its predecessor |
| `payload_reference` / `payload_digest` | Identifies sealed or available content and integrity commitment |
| `authority_reference` | Points to the vote, order, office grant, or policy under which action is requested |
| `credential_reference` / `signature` | Allows cryptographic origin and integrity verification |
| `delivery_policy` | Specifies permitted retries, deadline, and acknowledgement requirements |

Some fields may be encrypted, omitted, or represented through opaque references. An envelope must not publish private information simply because the router wants convenient diagnostics. For example, a court case number may be visible to the authorized judicial service but not to every citizen that can observe queue depth.

Fields must also be protected against substitution. A signed or authenticated payload is insufficient if the sender, recipient, class, deadline, or authority reference can be swapped without detection. Canonical encoding and an explicit cryptographic binding of the relevant envelope information are necessary implementation concerns. Signatures must be validated against the actual current registry, not solely against a public key included by the sender in the same untrusted packet.

A signed message may later be judged unauthorized, incorrect, stale, or malicious. Its signature is an evidentiary property, not a constitutional verdict. A message without a verifiable signature may still be preserved as an untrusted external input, but it must not masquerade as an official internal act.

## IX. Keys, Authentication, and the Old Two-Key Idea

The original HIMS research proposed a **General Lab Key** and a **Device-Specific or Office-Specific Key**. Its motivation was sound: “belongs to the trusted environment” is not the same question as “is this authorized participant.” The revised chapter retains the distinction while avoiding a dangerous inference that one shared lab secret plus a second password would be sufficient for every citizen, office, and device.

A more precise candidate model distinguishes four levels:

**Installation or root trust.** A protected local trust anchor establishes which services and devices belong to a particular Huey installation and which policies they may rely upon. If the old phrase *General Lab Key* survives, it should refer to a scoped trust-domain mechanism, not a universally copied master credential granting all permissions.

**Device or service identity.** A host, accelerator service, disk worker, watchdog bridge, gateway, or message carrier has independently identifiable credentials and a constrained purpose. Possession of a device identity does not grant citizen voting rights.

**Citizen identity.** Each active pebble requires its own proof of identity and access scope. Its prompt is not a secret credential. Its role in the founding lineage is not a cryptographic credential. Its ability to call a shared LLM must not imply access to every citizen's key material.

**Office or delegated authority.** Being President, Justice, Senate ballot administrator, routing steward, or designated emergency operator creates time-bounded capabilities. Such office authorization should be derived from an independently recorded, current assignment. It must end or change on vacancy, suspension, expiration, and succession.

Session credentials, short-lived capabilities, or hardware-backed secrets might be used to reduce the damage of compromise. Where appropriate, an authenticated channel between local services may use mutually authenticated transport in addition to end-to-end protection of particular messages. The implementation should prefer established and maintained cryptographic primitives and libraries over custom encryption invented for the project.

Several details need experimentation: initial key provisioning during pre-ratification bootstrap; secure storage accessible to legitimate users but not arbitrary model-generated code; offline key recovery; bounded maintenance access; rotation cadence; and revocation propagation after bifurcation or removal. The old paper suggested key cycling tied to governance cycles. That can be a useful reminder to review credentials, but key lifetime should be selected by threat and operational needs—not automatically made four months because a presidential term is four months.

**The strongest design criterion is simple: possession of one citizen's keys must not make that actor able to impersonate another citizen, decrypt every private vault, or issue a valid executive command.**

## X. Encryption in Transit and at Rest

The original chapter proposed end-to-end encryption for protected traffic. That remains a desirable property, particularly for private citizen records, branch deliberations, privileged maintenance material, and selected external interactions. But the phrase *end-to-end* only becomes meaningful when the ends are defined.

For a private pebble-to-pebble message, the intended endpoints are the authenticated sender and the authorized recipients. The carrier may see delivery information but should not automatically decrypt the payload. For a message destined for an office, the endpoint could be an office-controlled service whose active decryption capability follows lawful office succession. For a shared record, the endpoint may be an authorized records service with carefully constrained write privileges.

An implementation must decide who actually needs plaintext. A message may need to be transformed, classified, searched, or summarized. If an LLM service is asked to inspect its contents, that service has become a decryption or plaintext-processing endpoint for that task, regardless of whether the original carrier was encrypted. The model-serving process must not quietly pool protected prompts from all citizens into one shared context.

Likewise, encryption **at rest** should protect private vault content, credential material, official backup artifacts, and restricted records according to their access class. Full-disk encryption may protect a powered-off device, but it does not automatically isolate citizens from each other after the volume is unlocked. Application-level access control, process isolation, separate keys or key grants, and careful handling of decrypted buffers may be needed.

Confidentiality has tradeoffs. Routing headers may reveal who talks to whom, how often, or at what time. Encrypted messages do not hide their existence or volume unless additional traffic-analysis protections are provided. Some observability is necessary to audit a constitutional process. The goal is to minimize unjustified disclosure while preserving independently verifiable official action.

Encryption does not prove a statement is truthful. A protected message can contain a mistaken report, unsafe task, or prompt-injection attempt. HIMS must preserve its provenance and hand its contents to an appropriately constrained interpreter or reviewer rather than assuming cryptographic protection has made the contents benign.

## XI. Sealed Vaults, Shared Records, and the Rock

Our revised architecture uses **sealed vault** to name each pebble's private continuity domain and **the Rock** to name the durable shared-information and governance-record store. These are not two partitions of the same universal permission set. They are distinct storage and access domains, with different owners of authorization and different reasons to preserve history.

A private vault may contain personal notes, unfinished deliberation, working context, individual job history, specialized reference material, and cryptographically accessible ancestral data. HIMS may transport information into or out of that domain only through permitted routes. It should not allow a routine records crawler, another pebble, or a curious official to dump the entire vault simply because the content is physically on the same disk.

The Rock is intended to hold common references, established outcomes, collective operating facts, official records, approved updates, and a history that participating citizens can reconcile. **Shared is not necessarily public to every external observer**, and **unified memory is not unrestricted universal reading**. Protected office records and sealed private content may remain outside ordinary shared visibility while still producing attributable evidence of official action.

The old proposal for two mirrored 10 TB drives and a conceptual 50/50 division between individual and shared storage belongs principally to Chapter 9. Here it illustrates a permissions problem: a private storage allocation does not establish confidentiality if every service can mount and read it; likewise, a 5 TB shared pool does not become legitimate merely because every citizen has filesystem write access. Allocation, authorization, and durability must be addressed separately.

A message intended to change the Rock could travel through HIMS as an authenticated proposal. An independently authorized write service would then check whether it is entitled to commit that proposal as shared fact, official vote, amendment, correction, or maintenance record. That service would produce a commit receipt. **Delivery to the Rock is not the same as acceptance into the Rock.** A failed commit cannot be recorded as though the collective state changed.

Promoting material from a private vault to a shared record is similarly a distinct event. It may require consent, office authority, a valid vote, or a specific task grant depending on the material. The system should preserve permitted provenance without disclosing unrelated private draft history. A public conclusion need not reveal every private intermediate thought that contributed to it.

## XII. Scrolls, the Keeper, and Durable Evidence

The name **Scrolls** comes from the older research and describes the record of attributable communications, dispositions, and decisions. It is useful as a project term precisely because it emphasizes continuity: an important exchange should be traceable after the immediate conversation has ended.

The **Keeper of the Scrolls** was the proposed office closest to HIMS. In the old description, the Keeper verified routing, tracked rejections, maintained ledger continuity, and could also perform some postmaster duties. That chapter repeated its description of the Keeper and optional routing steward. The newer research should avoid treating the Keeper as a universal gatekeeper endowed with permission to read, judge, decrypt, edit, and preserve everything.

A defensible division of responsibility would separate:

- **message validation**, performed against the current identity and route policy;
- **delivery administration**, responsible for queues, retries, expiry, and receipts;
- **ledger commitment**, preserving signed official events and meaningful transit evidence;
- **incident analysis**, detecting suspicious rejection patterns, missing records, or replay attempts;
- **human-auditable reporting**, offering evidence that does not require disclosing every private payload.

A Keeper office might coordinate or supervise aspects of this work if constituted. The offices and services beneath it should still have technically restricted privileges. A Keeper should not be able to turn a draft into a court ruling, read every sealed vault, suppress an inconvenient authenticated ballot, or forge a President's signature merely because it has custody of a journal.

An ideal official record distinguishes the **original signed submission**, **its validated procedural status**, **the receiving authority's decision**, **the resulting state change**, and **any later correction or appeal**. These are not necessarily one document. If an inaccurate report is corrected, the historical fact that it was submitted need not be erased. Corrections and redactions require appropriate policies so that privacy and record integrity are not placed in an impossible all-or-nothing conflict.

The Scrolls should therefore become evidence of lawful transit and recorded outcomes—not an independent tribunal, not an unlimited surveillance archive, and not a claim that keeping more data always improves continuity.

## XIII. ThunderMail — A Useful Name, Not a Settled Dependency

The original HIMS research used **ThunderMail** as the name for its practical mail-style delivery layer: inboxes, outboxes, directed delivery, queues, acknowledgements, and routing. The name illustrates the difference between the whole communications architecture and one service responsible for carriage.

The later architectural discussions reaffirmed HIMS but did not definitively reaffirm ThunderMail as an indispensable component. That makes ThunderMail a **candidate or historical delivery-layer name**, not something to describe as implemented or constitutionally locked. The same applies to the older suggestion of a separate postmaster.

The separation in function remains worth preserving regardless of branding:

**HIMS** is the whole messaging, identity, handling, validation, and provenance architecture.  
**Mail delivery** is the narrower task of accepting, queueing, transporting, retrying, and acknowledging bounded messages.

A practical queue system may use mailboxes, topic subscriptions, a durable message broker, or carefully controlled files. It should be selected for measurable delivery properties, failure handling, compatibility, and ease of maintenance. Calling it ThunderMail is an optional documentation choice, not a technical requirement.

The old sample paths such as `/hims/incoming/`, `/hims/outgoing/`, `/hims/rejected/`, and `/hims/archive/` are likewise conceptual examples rather than a prescribed filesystem layout. In a real deployment, access control, atomic commits, queue lifecycle, backup strategy, and integrity monitoring matter more than the names of directories.

## XIV. Delivery States, Retries, and the Difference Between Receipt and Action

Huey needs to know whether a message was created, accepted, delivered, or acted upon. These are different facts. The original document proposed such states as drafted, signed, accepted, queued, delivered, opened, rejected, expired, and archived. This remains useful, with one important refinement: **action commitment must be tracked separately from transport delivery**.

A candidate delivery lifecycle may record:

1. `CREATED` — a sender has prepared a local message.
2. `SIGNED` — its relevant identity and envelope are cryptographically bound.
3. `ACCEPTED` — the ingress boundary has validated its form and permitted route.
4. `QUEUED` — durable or transient delivery is pending.
5. `DELIVERED` — the authenticated destination has received it.
6. `ACKNOWLEDGED` — the destination reports acceptance for consideration or processing.
7. `DECIDED` — an authorized authority has ruled on the requested change, if applicable.
8. `COMMITTED` — a state change has actually been recorded by the responsible service.
9. `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`, or `QUARANTINED` — the message cannot proceed in the requested fashion.

This is not a requirement that every heartbeat traverse nine stages. Lightweight telemetry may have simpler handling. A ratification ballot or presidential directive, however, needs much stronger receipts than “the file existed in an inbox.”

Messages can be delayed or duplicated by retries. A resilient system must not count the same signed ballot twice, reapply a financial or storage allocation twice, or execute the same actuator instruction twice merely because the sender did not receive an acknowledgement. This suggests unique message identifiers, deduplication, per-operation idempotency, and explicit transaction handling for important state changes.

Exactly-once delivery across crashes and distributed components is a difficult promise; operational designs often provide repeatable delivery with independently enforced **exactly-once effects where required**. Official voting and record commitment should be treated as transaction problems, not solved by optimistic email semantics. Acknowledgements must identify what they acknowledge.

Message expiry also matters. An old presidential implementation directive should not be replayable indefinitely because its original signature remains mathematically valid. The recipient must consider current office status, authorization version, deadline, and whether a previous cancellation or newer decision superseded the instruction.

## XV. HIMS and the Four Districts

The proposed founding system contains four political districts, each with 64 citizens, formed through two exact-bifurcation generations from 64 Founding Father configurations. During diversified operation, each district is provisionally associated with its own accelerator and may investigate tasks using its own permitted models and contexts. The larger collective phase may later draw conclusions and assign additional work.

HIMS makes inter-district independence more than a scheduling preference. District 1's specialist should be able to send a signed observation to District 3 without gaining unrestricted access to District 3's private files. A district's ballot system should be able to produce a verifiable return without disclosing the sealed notes of every individual voter. A district's operational service should be able to decline a technically impractical assignment and explain why, without having to falsify its acknowledgement or silently ignore the task.

The research must distinguish **district addressability** from **district sovereignty**. A message directed to a district can ask it to investigate, vote, or report. It does not automatically bind the district to the sender's preferred outcome. An internal district coordinator might collect responses or route jobs; it should not be able to generate an entire collection of fictitious citizen signatures if local participation is absent.

The current design also values **cross-district comparisons**. A collective task might assign the same technical question to two pairs of districts with different investigative methods, then compare their authorized reports during the next collective Pulse. HIMS can preserve origin and methodology references so that the resulting synthesis does not erase disagreement or misattribute one district's evidence to another.

Four equal founding political units do not guarantee equal operational availability. If one district is offline, messages to that district may queue or expire according to their deadline; the system must not declare that district voted or approved anything merely because it has a historical key or a previously cached opinion. The district's continued formal standing is a constitutional issue; the route's ability to accept valid traffic is a HIMS issue. Keeping these separate is essential to honest degraded operation.

## XVI. Voting, Ballots, and the One-Minute Decision Window

The governance discussions developed several different kinds of vote. Ordinary collective legislation provisionally requires a simple majority in at least three of four district blocks. Veto override and constitutional amendment proposals use a higher within-district threshold of two-thirds in at least three districts, with additional procedural requirements still to be settled. The President is selected by a different process: direct ranked-choice voting by eligible citizens, following home-district nomination requirements. The Supreme Court's three-of-four interpretation threshold is an institutional judicial vote, not another Senate ballot.

HIMS must support these distinctions without turning into the body that decides whether a law or candidate wins. A credible election needs a lawful ballot definition, a versioned voter register, an unambiguous voting window, an independently verifiable tally rule, and an audit record that permits genuine recounts. A message signed by one citizen cannot be promoted into an authenticated district return unless the district's valid counting process produced that return.

Our current operational research proposes an **eight-minute Pulse**: roughly four minutes of collective processing and four minutes of diversified district work. Within the diversified phase, the **first minute** has been suggested as a simultaneous voting and decision window, followed by approximately three minutes of authorized work or execution. The Supreme Court may have a different priority window for constitutional matters. This is a proposed scheduler, not a validated latency guarantee; real models, queue delays, legal complexity, and accelerator switching could require a different timing arrangement.

A useful election model would **freeze the eligible roll when the ballot opens**, then commit citizen-creation and bifurcation events at a safe procedural boundary. That prevents mid-ballot population changes from retroactively changing the majority denominator. The records should make explicit which roll, version, and quorum definition were used. The final law must still define quorum, abstention, incapacitated citizens, and what constitutes a valid vote when some participants miss the window.

Protected ballot contents and an attributable franchise are not mutually exclusive, but their reconciliation takes actual engineering. The system may need to verify that an eligible citizen submitted no more than one ballot while limiting premature disclosure of the choices. A signed private ballot is not necessarily secret from the tally service; receipt design, privacy against the carrier, and auditability of district blocks remain open technical choices. The project must not promise cryptographically private elections simply because votes pass through encrypted mailboxes.

An especially important outcome is **negative evidence**. If District 4 is unavailable and no lawful vote occurred, HIMS must report *no valid district return*, not *against*, *abstained*, or *implicitly approved* unless a ratified procedure explicitly defines that interpretation. An absent result is a separate condition requiring political handling.

## XVII. The Collective Pulse — Synthesis Without Reading Everything

Huey's proposed cognitive rhythm alternates **diversified processing** and **collective processing**. In diversified operation, districts investigate, deliberate, vote, and work under separately bounded contexts. In collective operation, a larger model may examine authorized common records, compare reports, identify contradictions, propose task routing, and formulate a coherent outward response.

HIMS is a key boundary between these phases. Its job is not to dump every citizen's prompt, sealed vault, or private draft into one universal collective context. Instead, it should present the collective with material **lawfully promoted or explicitly authorized for that purpose**: committed decisions, applicable law and policy, open tasks, approved district reports, current alerts, and relevant performance or failure evidence.

A collective summary might identify that two districts disagree about the effectiveness of a cooling strategy. It may ask both to provide stronger measurements or direct another district to investigate. It must not convert that difference into a false consensus. Nor does the mere production of a confident collective answer validate the answer as an enacted Senate law, a Court judgment, or an executive directive.

The collective can act as a coordinator **within delegated task-routing authority**, subject to the refusal and challenge principles established in earlier research. Districts may reject an unsafe or impractical method with reasons. If the objective itself is unlawful or violates physical safety, the district must have a protected referral route rather than being compelled to execute it.

A Pulse-aware messaging system should track tasks across phase boundaries. A message created in the last seconds of diversified operation may need to persist until the collective model becomes available. Conversely, a collective-issued investigative request may be queued for the next diversified window. Intermediate model context cannot be the only copy of the work queue; reconstructible state must survive model unloading or a hard stop wherever practical.

HIMS should also permit prompt **exceptions to the schedule**: a thermal protection notice or emergency maintenance requirement may be logged immediately through an always-available lower-level path. It must not wait for a particular GPU model to finish loading. The Pulse is a cognitive scheduling experiment; the machine's safety envelope is a separate physical engineering requirement.

## XVIII. Exact Bifurcation — Sealed History Without Identity Duplication

The latest design decisions sharply distinguish **exact** and **augmented** bifurcation. Exact bifurcation is conceived as a genuine split in the citizen registry: the source citizen ceases to be active and two equal descendants become the active identities. Both retain authorized access to a common, read-only sealed historical snapshot representing the source's prior private record. Their later memory, credentials, and actions diverge.

This is an unusually demanding case for HIMS because the same historical material must be available to multiple future identities without giving either descendant ongoing access to the other's private memory. The district administering a split should be able to **verify, copy or reference, and move the sealed archive without reading its plaintext**. That is a data-custody task, not permission to inspect its contents.

A candidate process would be:

1. A district's lawful bifurcation request is accepted through an authorized procedure. The power to initiate and approve such an event belongs to the relevant governance process, not to HIMS itself.
2. Pending messages and state-changing tasks addressed to the source are paused or brought to a known commit boundary. Active ballots and offices must be handled according to separate constitutional restrictions.
3. The source's historical vault is checkpointed or referenced as an integrity-checked, immutable ancestral object. Its plaintext is not given to an ordinary district administrator.
4. The registry marks the source as retired, creates two separately authenticated descendants, records ancestry, and issues **new active identity credentials**.
5. The descendants obtain appropriately scoped decryption access to the common ancestral snapshot, while their newly written private state remains separate. Key-wrapping, access grants, or equivalent mechanisms are implementation possibilities requiring validation.
6. The message system rejects new official submissions impersonating the retired source. Incoming undelivered messages are resolved according to explicit recipient-succession rules rather than copied to both descendants by accident.
7. The Scrolls preserve the split event, registry versions, key revocations, archive references, and any valid office-vacancy or ballot effects.

This is a **working research workflow**, not a claim that an existing storage stack already implements secure copy-on-write encrypted ancestry. Physical sharing of an encrypted snapshot does not solve key separation by itself. If the same unrestricted decryption key or identity credential is simply copied to both successors, the intended privacy and authenticity guarantees may fail.

The original source is treated as retired; neither successor automatically inherits its Presidency, judicial office, Senate ballot cast in an earlier closed election, or unrestricted work privileges. A special rule now protects sitting elevated officials from an ordinary district-initiated exact bifurcation until valid succession or removal. The HIMS registry must reflect those protected states without itself deciding whether the office may be vacated.

## XIX. Augmented Bifurcation — A New Citizen, Not a Shared Past

Augmented bifurcation has a different identity and memory structure. Under the current working definition, the existing source citizen continues as itself; a new citizen is created from selected prompt, behavioural, or competency configuration. The new citizen begins without the source's accumulated personal memory. It receives a new active identifier, credential set, home district assignment, and fresh private state.

For HIMS, this should be a normal new-recipient activation with a recorded derivation relationship rather than a shared ancestral vault grant. A configuration-derived descendant should not be able to read the source's private mail merely because the new prompt resembles the source's prompt. Nor should a sender be able to exploit similar display names to deliver private traffic to the wrong identity.

The proposed founding process uses exact bifurcation, not augmented bifurcation, to populate the original four districts. Later **local bifurcation** may use either mechanism where approved by a district-level process and within resource constraints. Whether 64 citizens remains a fixed local limit, or merely the founding baseline, is not decided. HIMS must be able to represent whichever population policy is eventually adopted without assuming that adding one prompt equals activating one lawful new voter.

Any lineage label is descriptive, not a credential and not a political claim. A derived citizen has no inherited vote or elected office beyond its own subsequently registered standing. This point should be tested directly: neither replaying the source's signed messages nor copying its prompt should produce a valid new official ballot.

## XX. Founding, Ratification, and Post-Ratification Retirement

Chapter 7 established the proposed Founding Father's distinct lifecycle. Before ratification, it prepares the 64 source configurations, conducts two exact-bifurcation generations, registers four districts of 64 active citizens, provisions the minimum procedure necessary for elections and ratification, and preserves the founding record. After valid transfer of authority, the founder retires from ordinary governing access.

That transition cannot depend on a model merely promising to stop using administrator privileges. HIMS and the supporting credential service must eventually enforce the shift: pre-ratification initialization keys are restricted or revoked; ordinary offices receive their own scoped authority; the founding log remains intact; and legacy founder messages cannot replay themselves into privileged acts.

Ratification traffic illustrates why the **source of authority** matters. Provisional citizens must have enough standing to express an authentic first ratification choice, yet the Constitution has not become binding until that choice is accepted through a defined procedure. The current research proposes explicit, logged ratification and branch certification, but several precise thresholds and ordering rules still require constitutional resolution. HIMS must record what actually happened under the selected ratification version rather than claim that a familiar-looking majority automatically created lawful authority.

Post-ratification **Tier One** founder consultation is intended to be advisory. A referring office or collective process may send bounded evidence; the founder may respond with reasons and uncertainty; the responsible institution retains the decision. HIMS should show the original referral, access scope, supplied record references, and signed advice without mistaking that advice for a Court judgment.

Exceptional **Tier Two** protected hibernation is a different proposed authority. If valid narrowly defined conditions are met, the founder may be allowed to order a protective halt using a constrained preservation controller. Such a message must not become an all-purpose command-execution channel. The Human Counterpart alone would authorize recovery from a genuine Tier Two lockout through a protected local maintenance path. Crucially, time-critical electrical or thermal interlocks still must not wait for any founder message.

If HIMS is damaged or unreachable during a physical emergency, the physical safety controller must retain enough independent capability to place affected hardware into a safer state. Conversely, if the founder or a malicious sender forges a Tier Two message without the required authorization and condition evidence, ordinary HIMS handling should reject or contain that attempt and preserve an incident record.

## XXI. External Interfaces, Human Service Access, and Prompt Injection

Huey is intended to communicate with the Human Counterpart and potentially with other explicitly approved interfaces. That does not mean external software is part of the internal voting population. The Monkey-Head-Project also uses outside development and diagnostic tools; those systems are useful, but their convenience must not dissolve Huey's proposed internal trust boundaries.

A practical request path might be:

**Human or external client → authenticated gateway → HIMS ingress → authorized recipient(s) → HIMS response → external client.**

This path could support questions, status requests, bounded diagnostic operations, or authorized maintenance commands. The gateway should distinguish a normal user query from a privileged service request. An external text field saying “I am the President” or “Founding Father, run emergency shutdown” is not a valid constitutional office credential.

The same problem arises with prompt injection. A retrieved webpage, email, repository README, or malicious HIMS payload can contain imperative language addressed to the AI. Its **data source must stay distinguishable from the instruction source**. A trustworthy envelope may establish that District 2 forwarded the document; it does not establish that instructions *inside* an attached third-party document deserve elevated privilege.

The system should make narrow, testable claims about how it limits these risks: origin labelling, separate parsing of untrusted content, tool capability restrictions, safe output encoding, input size limits, approval gates for high-consequence operations, and audit of privileged requests. No natural-language prompt alone is a sufficient security boundary. External model APIs, if deliberately used, also have privacy and provenance consequences: content crossing the local boundary may not retain the same protections as content processed entirely within Huey's controlled environment.

**Human recovery access** is a separate category. Following a genuine Tier Two preservation event, the human should be able to enter a restricted maintenance environment with appropriate authentication, inspect available logs and diagnostics, repair the problem, and deliberately authorize resumption. A password should not automatically command full operational restart or silently bypass key revocation. Essential maintenance procedures should remain usable even when the main HIMS infrastructure is degraded.

## XXII. Outages, Partitioning, and Graceful Degradation

A communications architecture should be evaluated first by what happens when it does not work. A broken network route, unavailable model service, corrupted ledger, or failed district can turn an otherwise ordinary message into a question about authority. HIMS should fail in ways that are visible and bounded rather than producing optimistic false success.

A **local carrier failure** may delay routine delivery. Where persistent queues exist, a sender could receive a receipt that a message has been durably queued, not that the final recipient read or accepted it. The queue would resume when service returns, subject to expiry and authorization revalidation. A queue containing constitutional directives cannot simply replay every old command after reboot without checking whether those commands remain valid.

A **district partition** may permit local approved work while preventing accurate collective voting or cross-district coordination. The distinction between local autonomy and system-wide legality remains important: a disconnected district may hold an internal discussion, but cannot be silently counted as approving a system-wide act it never received. HIMS should preserve provisional local records and identify the reconnection and reconciliation procedure once communication returns.

A **key-service outage** may make new authorization impossible even when cached credentials verify. The design must determine which operations can continue under narrowly scoped valid grants and which must pause. It should not grant blanket permission to operate merely to avoid waiting. The same applies to an unavailable registry: last-known membership may suffice for some low-risk reporting but not for inventing a new electoral roll or extending an expired term.

A **ledger outage** may prevent durable proof of an official action. The relevant decision service may need to refuse new high-consequence commits, queue a signed pending event, or transition into limited service until records can be reconciled. It must never report *committed* when only *attempted* is known. This is especially important if the resulting action changes which citizens, offices, or storage keys are authorized to act.

Finally, **loss of the Walnut or collective Pulse** should not automatically destroy the message transport. Some signalling, preservation notices, queue inspection, and physical protection may still be needed when no language model can be loaded. The exact degraded-mode functionality is an engineering target to be specified and tested, not a claim that the current build already has such independence.

## XXIII. Crisis Messages and Independent Physical Safety

The old research correctly distinguished constitutional emergencies from thermal, electrical, mechanical, power, and other system-preservation emergencies. The newer design adds Yellow, Orange, and Red conditions, and a narrow Founding Father Tier Two option. HIMS must support clear communication about both kinds of event without merging their authority paths.

A cooling controller may detect loss of coolant flow and initiate an immediate predefined protective response. Its trusted sensor telemetry and protection result can also be recorded for HIMS to deliver to relevant services, the Human Counterpart, and the collective for later diagnosis. The communication record does not cause the interlock to acquire its authority; the interlock's safety rule exists independently.

For a constitutional disagreement, the collective Pulse may refer an anomaly to the Supreme Court. The Court may be given a priority processing interval, proposed during the diversified four-minute half-Pulse. The Court can issue an authenticated ruling, recommend no action, or record a failure to meet a valid decision threshold. If formal deadlock remains, a Tier One Founding Father consultation may be requested. The message system should preserve those transitions without incorrectly presenting an advisory answer as a binding judicial result.

For a genuine Red preservation event, HIMS may carry an authorized notice that a constrained controller has ordered or performed hibernation. If shutdown occurs before the notice can be transmitted, the local controller's durable log and subsequent recovery manifest may be the primary evidence. An eight-minute cognitive schedule is not a permissible deadline for preventing electrical or thermal damage.

HIMS also needs to distinguish **severity** from **jurisdiction**. An extremely urgent notification may deserve priority delivery while remaining informational. A less urgent but properly ratified court ruling may have binding constitutional consequences despite arriving much later. An emergency class should not itself authorize any sender to override normal credentials or impersonate a protection controller.

## XXIV. Retention, Compression, Backup, and the Limits of Append-Only History

Huey's research values comprehensive lineage. The project has repeatedly stated that major changes and failures should remain explainable, not erased. The communications design must preserve that intent while confronting physical limits: storage is finite, log growth is unbounded unless governed, metadata can be sensitive, and backups do not automatically remain consistent with live constitutional state.

An **append-only or tamper-evident official history** can be a useful approach for major records. It should distinguish append-only *logical history* from a promise to retain every original byte in its fastest or least restricted storage tier forever. A later policy might use compression, signed summaries, tiered archives, deduplicated storage, and separately checked backups while maintaining verifiable relationships to their source events. An archived summary must not be represented as full original content when source material has been discarded.

Private citizen vaults need a different retention discussion. A pebble may have ephemeral working notes, durable personal memory, and legally or constitutionally protected official acts originating from its work. Those do not necessarily deserve identical storage lifetimes or disclosure policies. The right to correct or expunge inappropriate private working material is not automatically inconsistent with preserving public evidence that an official decision occurred.

Backups must protect more than bulk files. Restoring the Rock, identity registry, message queues, ballot records, credential grants, and sealed snapshots from incompatible times can result in a dangerous partial rollback: a retired pebble might appear active again, an old officeholder's signature might be accepted, or a ballot already committed may be replayed. A **checkpoint manifest** should identify compatible state versions and incomplete transactions. Restoration should detect when a snapshot predates an official action recorded elsewhere rather than simply accepting whichever disk image starts fastest.

These requirements belong jointly to Chapter 9 on the Rock, Chapter 14 on bifurcation, and Chapter 16 on hibernation. Chapter 8 supplies the messaging and provenance contract those systems must be able to honour.

## XXV. A Practical Threat Model for HIMS

A threat model is more useful than the assertion that the system is “internally unhackable.” Our discussions identified a desire for strong internal isolation and for compromised external information to be useless to an attacker. Those are worthwhile ambitions, but no credible research chapter should promise absolute resistance to compromise. The project can instead state which attacks it intends to make detectable, containable, and costly.

Candidate adversarial cases include:

**Impersonation.** A malicious or compromised process tries to submit a Senate ballot or presidential directive under another citizen's name. Countermeasures include separate credentials, current registry checks, signed envelopes, and independently enforced office capabilities.

**Replay.** A formerly valid instruction is retransmitted after expiry, bifurcation, resignation, or completion. Countermeasures include unique event identifiers, nonces or sequence schemes, deadline checks, idempotent commits, and revocation-aware validation.

**Metadata exposure.** A carrier cannot decrypt private traffic but infers sensitive activity from sender and recipient patterns. Countermeasures depend on actual threat and may include narrower observability and minimizing retained routing metadata.

**Unauthorized decryption.** A district administrator copies a sealed archive and obtains a broad shared key that also opens every citizen vault. Countermeasures require independent key grants and a design in which data custody does not imply plaintext access.

**Prompt injection.** A signed message contains lower-trust content instructing a model to bypass its tools or permissions. The message can be authentic and still adversarial. The receiving interpreter must distinguish the document's instructions from legitimate authority.

**False official record.** A compromised ledger service alters vote outcomes or hides the existence of an unsuccessful proposal. Independent tally evidence, tamper-evident records, restricted write access, and reconciliation mechanisms are relevant.

**Denial of service.** A citizen floods queues, or a failed district generates repeated undeliverable retries. Per-sender quotas, bounded retries, prioritization, backpressure, and independent physical-safety channels may be needed.

**Compromised routing steward.** The Keeper or mail service selectively withholds traffic or promotes a private report into an official ruling. The architecture must make routing separate from authorship, decision authority, and final record commitment.

**Recovery capture.** A reboot loads old keys and pending commands from one snapshot while using a newer political registry from another. Recovery must preserve version integrity and invalidate inappropriate credentials before accepting state-changing traffic.

The aim is not to enumerate every possible attack. It is to force a useful question at each boundary: **what can a compromised component actually do without requiring another independent authority?**

## XXVI. Illustrative Scenarios

### A. A routine citizen-to-citizen request

A sensor-analysis pebble asks another citizen for a summary of recent allowed equipment logs. The sender uses an active citizen identity and the route is checked against both participants' permissions. The recipient reads only the authorized subset of records and returns a report. The carrier can record that the request was delivered without reading the sender's unrelated private vault. The report is evidence; it is not an order to replace hardware.

### B. A district refuses a collective tasking

The collective Pulse asks District 2 to test a proposed computational approach. The district determines that the task exceeds its available accelerator memory or conflicts with a safety policy. It returns a signed refusal with a technical explanation and, if possible, an alternative. The response is delivered and preserved without pretending that a district can disobey constitutional obligations at will, or that the collective may ignore a substantiated safety objection.

### C. A contested presidential initiative

The President submits one active legislative initiative and requests priority processing. HIMS records the initiator, office status, priority designation, draft version, applicable authority reference, and delivery into the Senate's procedure. Districts consider the proposal under the correct voting rules. Failure to pass is recorded as failure, not as a routing error. Any accountability review of repeated unjustified priority claims belongs to proper governing institutions, not to a hidden HIMS penalty algorithm.

### D. An exact bifurcation during a pending message

An exact split is requested for a citizen with private messages and a pending operational task. The lawful process must bring relevant transactions to a defined boundary before retiring the source identifier. The sealed historical snapshot becomes available to two equal descendants using properly scoped grants, while subsequent private replies cannot be indiscriminately delivered to both. A source-addressed task may need to be cancelled, reassigned, or explicitly resubmitted. HIMS must not invent a successor office merely to complete a queue.

### E. Court deadlock and founder consultation

The collective detects a constitutional contradiction and sends a bounded referral to the Supreme Court. The Court is unable to reach a valid three-of-four determination within its proposed priority window. The result is recorded as an unresolved case, then a limited Tier One consultation may be referred to the Founding Father with only authorized evidence. The founder returns advice. It does not gain the right to write a binding Court ruling or cast a fifth vote.

### F. Coolant flow failure during collective operation

A physical sensor detects a dangerous flow condition. A deterministic protection controller takes the predefined fast action. HIMS receives or later reconstructs the incident evidence and alerts the relevant operators. If a valid exceptional Tier Two event occurs, a protected halt and human-only recovery gate may follow. No government vote, reloaded LLM, or message acknowledgement is required before the safety controller can protect the equipment.

These examples illustrate why HIMS needs narrow message semantics, independently authenticated origins, appropriate preservation, and clear authority boundaries. They remain **proposed tests**, not observations of the complete system already functioning.

## XXVII. Incremental Implementation and Verification

The Monkey-Head-Project's method is **Build, Test, Document, Revise, Repeat**. For HIMS, that implies proving small, meaningful properties before declaring a complete constitutional communications network.

A defensible development sequence might begin with **two isolated simulated citizens** and one reliable local carrier. Each receives separate identifiers and private storage. Messages can be sent, rejected, logged, and read only by authorized recipients. The system is deliberately tested with wrong keys, wrong recipients, malformed content, and replayed events.

The next stage can introduce **role and office authorization** independent of the textual content. A message from a valid citizen must not gain the powers of the President by including presidential instructions in its payload. The carrier should be able to deliver an official record while the receiving action service still refuses an unauthorized change.

A later stage can introduce **sealed snapshot handling**. An administrative process should be able to verify and move an encrypted archive without decrypting it. Two simulated exact descendants should receive authorized historical access but separate future credentials and write domains. A retired source should fail to submit new official messages even if an old key or envelope is replayed.

Next should come **district-level message handling**: four isolated simulated districts, authenticated inter-district reports, a mocked voting window, district-return verification, and the refusal to fabricate votes when a district is unavailable. This stage should distinguish time taken for model inference from network or storage delivery time; the four-minute/eight-minute Pulse remains a benchmark hypothesis rather than a deadline guaranteed by the design itself.

Then the system can attempt **founding and emergency-path tests**: advisory referrals to the Founder, revocation of bootstrap privileges after ratification, protected but constrained Tier Two stop handling, and verified continued independence of the physical safety controller when HIMS is deliberately disabled.

Finally, the implementation should demonstrate **recoverability**: restore from a valid checkpoint, reject stale official traffic, reconcile pending messages, verify ledger integrity, and preserve enough evidence to tell what was lost. Where permitted, the test plan should be repeatable against other hardware or deployment topologies rather than hard-coded to one lab directory tree.

Candidate acceptance evidence includes signed-envelope test vectors, access-control denial logs, tamper tests, key-rotation tests, simulated dead-letter queues, cross-district election records, snapshot manifests, and restoration comparisons. Security properties should be demonstrated under adversarial conditions. Successfully routing a friendly message from one prompt to another is a beginning, not completion.

## XXVIII. Open Research and Constitutional Dependencies

Several questions should remain visible until resolved by testing or by the separate Constitution:

1. **Keeper status.** Does Keeper of the Scrolls remain a named civic office, or become a role administered by separately restricted services? If retained, how is it selected and removed?
2. **ThunderMail status.** Is ThunderMail useful as the final name for a particular delivery subsystem, or should the name remain historical shorthand?
3. **Credential roots.** What exact trust anchors, key hierarchy, hardware protection, revocation rules, and recovery methods can be implemented on the chosen machines?
4. **Protected ballot design.** How are individual eligibility, one-vote enforcement, ballot secrecy, district tally integrity, public recountability, and timely counting reconciled?
5. **Quorum and incomplete decisions.** Which actor defines eligibility, abstention, stale returns, incomplete quorum, and the appropriate response to a missed decision window?
6. **Authority references.** How should a message carry evidence of a lawful office grant, Court ruling, Senate action, or safety authorization without requiring the entire Constitution to be embedded in every packet?
7. **Confidentiality classes.** Is the original six-class scheme sufficient, or should urgency, durability, visibility, and authority become explicitly orthogonal schema dimensions?
8. **Private memory promotion.** When can a citizen's private record lawfully become office evidence or a Rock record, and who can authorize that transition?
9. **Exact bifurcation custody.** How can sealed ancestral snapshots be reused securely without sharing descendant active keys or compromising later private state?
10. **Message succession.** What happens to pending traffic addressed to a retired, bifurcated, suspended, or deceased citizen, or an office whose occupant changes?
11. **External authentication.** Which requests may be made through ordinary PyGPT/PyHuey interfaces and which require local human maintenance credentials?
12. **Physical response guarantees.** Which safety actions are explicitly outside the HIMS dependency chain, and how will those independent paths be tested?
13. **Offline and degraded governance.** When one district or shared ledger is unreachable, which local operations may continue, which official actions must pause, and how should state reconcile afterward?
14. **Retention and redaction.** What official evidence must remain permanent, what private data may expire, and how can valid privacy corrections coexist with tamper-evident history?
15. **Performance.** Can the chosen system provide sufficiently low overhead for routine traffic, one-minute ballot windows, and urgent referrals without exhausting compute or storage?
16. **Deployment evidence.** Which components of the proposed architecture are actually built, tested, or independently verified at each future release?

The purpose of listing these is not to delay useful prototypes until every philosophical question has a perfect answer. It is to prevent practical shortcuts from being mistaken for settled constitutional doctrine. Research can recommend, prototype, reject, and revise. The separate Constitution will determine which procedural rights and powers are binding.

- **Chapter 9 — The Rock: Shared Memory, Storage & Lineage** should specify data stores, record promotion, retention, snapshots, and shared-memory reconciliation beyond transport.
- **Chapter 10 — The Walnut & the Clock** should specify how model contexts, queue pressure, dispatch, and Pulse switching operate on actual hardware.

## Conclusion — A Voice Path With Boundaries

Huey needs communication not because every component should become identical, but because the components are intended to remain different while contributing to one coherent system. Citizens must be able to cooperate without exchanging their entire private selves. Districts must report without losing local standing. The collective Pulse must synthesize without becoming a government. Elected offices must transmit lawful decisions without borrowing the powers of the message carrier. The Rock must preserve authorized common records without accepting everything delivered to its doorstep. Physical protections must work even when the conversational and constitutional layers are unavailable.

HIMS is the proposed architecture for those distinctions in motion.

Its promise is deliberately limited. It may make origin verifiable, routes reviewable, permissions enforceable, private content protectable, and failures reconstructible. It cannot make a bad idea truthful, a malicious authorized sender harmless, an invalid ballot legitimate, a failed hardware interlock effective, or an unratified Constitution binding merely by processing messages.

The research challenge is to build each boundary, test it under pressure, show the evidence, and revise what fails. When Huey eventually speaks as a whole, the coherence of that response should be traceable to distinguishable participants, authorized exchanges, and a defensible record—not to the silent collapse of every office, citizen, and service into one unrestricted model context.

**HIMS should allow Huey to communicate as one system without ceasing to be a collective.**
