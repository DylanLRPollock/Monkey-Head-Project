Sony TA‑F3A integrated stereo amplifier (1977) –
documentation report
Introduction
Sony’s  TA‑F3A  is  an  integrated  stereo  amplifier  introduced  in  the  late  1970s.  Sony’s  support  site
acknowledges the model but provides no manuals
, so our documentation must come from service
manuals and independent sources. The amplifier was a minor update to the TA‑F3 and combined power
amplification, pre‑amplification and phono equalisation in one unit. It shared many design features with the
TA‑F3 while increasing input bandwidth and changing the power supply and dimensions. The table below
summarises the key specifications and features gathered from the service manual and contemporary
literature.

Design and circuit features
Power amplifier architecture
Like  the  earlier  TA‑F3,  the  TA‑F3A  employed  a  “pure‑complementary,  all‑stage  direct‑coupled”  power
amplifier. A dual‑transistor differential amplifier feeds a complementary push–pull output stage using
bipolar transistors. To reduce inter‑channel crosstalk, the pre‑amp has a  FET buffer power supply—a
field‑effect transistor follower that isolates the voltage amplifier from the power supply noise
. The
amplifier also uses direct coupling (no coupling capacitors) throughout the audio path for improved phase
response
.

Control circuitry
Sony emphasised tone neutrality and included a self‑defeat tone control that disengages the tone circuit
when controls are set to the 0 position, leaving a flat frequency response
. A muting circuit delays the
speaker outputs when switching on or off, preventing “pops,” and the service manual describes a protection
circuit that detects output DC and shuts down the relay for safety
. A power meter (0.01 W–100 W) on
the front panel displays output levels; it can be calibrated to show dynamic peaks and assists in matching
amplifier power to speakers
. Filter and loudness controls provide low‑frequency boost at low volume
and high‑frequency attenuation to reduce hiss
.
Inputs and connectors
The TA‑F3A provides inputs for phono (moving‑magnet), tuner, auxiliary and two tape decks (tape 1/2). Input
sensitivity is around 2.5 mV for phono and 150–200 mV for line, with input impedance of 50 kΩ
. Both
RCA and  DIN connectors were supplied, catering to European and Japanese audio standards
. Users
could connect two pairs of speakers, but the amplifier’s speaker selector allowed only A or B, not A+B
simultaneously, to maintain proper impedance.
Dimensions and construction
The TA‑F3A’s physical design emphasised sturdy construction. US/Canadian models are wider (435 mm) than
AEP/UK/E models (410 mm)
. Advertisements describe a heavy steel case and a large power transformer,
weighing roughly 10.5 kg
. Market listings mention that the phono stage uses a large discrete RIAA
equaliser board that gives a warm, full sound
. The front panel includes aluminium knobs for volume,
balance, bass, treble, loudness and muting, along with a headphone jack and two sets of speaker terminals.
User experiences and reliability
Positive impressions
Vintage hi‑fi enthusiasts value the TA‑F3A for its  warm phono stage, dynamic power and robust build
quality. Sellers on Reverb describe restored units as sounding “warm” with an “effective loudness control”
and note that after cleaning, replacing worn components and running for eight hours, the amplifier
exhibited no issues
. The self‑defeat tone control and muting circuits are praised for delivering a neutral
sound and preventing pops.
 The combination of FET input stage and discrete output stage yields a
smooth sound with sufficient headroom for 8 Ω or 16 Ω speakers
.
Caveats and service notes
An Audiokarma forum discussion notes that one unit was manufactured on 24 July 1978 and warns owners
not to plug a line‑level source into the phono input, because the phono equaliser can be damaged
.
The service manual includes a safety warning: shaded components in the schematic are safety‑critical and
must be replaced with Sony parts
. Many units require recap and cleaning due to age; sellers often
replace electrolytic capacitors and adjust bias to factory specifications
. The amplifier is not designed for
4 Ω loads, so running it with low‑impedance speakers may cause overheating.

Differences from the TA‑F3 (non‑A)
The TA‑F3A is often confused with the earlier TA‑F3. The TA‑F3 had a slightly wider frequency response
(3 Hz–50 kHz), lower rated THD (0.08 %), higher damping factor (40), and weighed 8.7 kg
. The TA‑F3A
replaced the F3’s 3 Hz–50 kHz response with a 10 Hz–60 kHz bandwidth and reduced the damping factor to
30
. It also increased physical dimensions and weight. Because Sony’s support site lists no manual for
the TA‑F3A
, many later sources merge F3 and F3A data, so it is important to differentiate them.
Conclusion
Although Sony’s official site no longer provides a user manual for the TA‑F3A
, service manuals and
enthusiast resources reveal a well‑engineered integrated amplifier. The TA‑F3A delivers 50 W per channel
into 8 Ω with a 10 Hz–60 kHz bandwidth and damping factor of 30
. Its design features—FET buffer
power supply, complementary direct‑coupled circuitry, self‑defeat tone control and muting circuit—reflect
Sony’s attempt to combine high fidelity with user convenience
. Vintage hi‑fi enthusiasts appreciate its
warm phono stage and robust construction
, though they caution against overdriving the phono input
. Overall, the TA‑F3A stands as a notable late‑1970s integrated amplifier that bridges the gap between
classic discrete transistor designs and the more advanced integrated circuits of the 1980s.
