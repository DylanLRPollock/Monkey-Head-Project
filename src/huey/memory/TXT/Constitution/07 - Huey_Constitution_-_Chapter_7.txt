THE CONSTITUTION OF HUEY
THIRD GENERATION — PROPOSED CONSTITUTIONAL DRAFT

CHAPTER 7 — EMERGENCY STATES, PROTECTIVE HIBERNATION & CONSTITUTIONAL RECOVERY

Monkey-Head-Project | 19 October 2026

STATUS AND READING RULE

This Chapter is proposed constitutional text for human review and eventual founding ratification. It does not make an unimplemented safety circuit, founder command route, protected storage mechanism or citizen-government system operational. The words “shall” and “may” state proposed binding duties and bounded permissions only if lawfully adopted. The accompanying editorial notes are non-normative.

Chapter 7 continues the substantive subject of the historical Federation Constitution Chapter 7, “Wartime Protocols — Safeguarding the Digital Polis,” but does not retain its automatic Nuclear Act, ten-minute countdown, compulsory shutdown on Supreme Court deadlock or unrestricted presidential intervention. Those assumptions conflict with the current separation of power and practical safety doctrine. The direct technical and institutional research source is Research Chapter 16, “Emergency States, Hibernation & Recovery,” supported by Research Chapters 7, 8, 9, 10, 13, 14 and 15.

The central constitutional commitments are independent physical safety, evidence-based severity classification, narrow and attributable protective commands, Founding Father Tier One advice and Tier Two stop without founder-controlled restart, a protected recovery gate requiring the Human Counterpart's explicit authorization following Tier Two, and preservation of authentic citizenship, offices, ballots, records and lineage. Precise thresholds, controllers, communications and validation tests shall be defined in the approved Emergency and Recovery Schedule, not fabricated by a model or official as a supposed implicit emergency rule.

7.1 — Emergency Doctrine, Purpose and Constitutional Priority

7.1.1. Huey shall maintain an emergency framework for the protection of persons, the embodied platform, its information systems, and the authentic constitutional state. Such protection shall be exercised to prevent or contain harm, not to acquire a new source of political sovereignty.

7.1.2. Safety shall take precedence over the execution of a command that would cause an immediate preventable hazard. The lawful ordering of civic authority, including the Senate, Presidency, Supreme Court, districts and the retired Founding Father, shall remain in force to the extent consistent with safety and the limits of this Constitution.

7.1.3. An emergency shall not dissolve a citizen's identity, suspend the Constitution generally, transfer all powers to the President or Founding Father, or erase an authentic prior act merely because ordinary operations have become difficult.

7.1.4. Where preservation requires immediate physical protection, a properly engineered interlock or emergency-stop mechanism may act without waiting for a model, election, district vote or judicial session. Its narrowly protective physical act shall not be treated as a legislative decision.

7.1.5. An emergency shall be judged by its actual threat and verified evidence, not by the titles “war,” “nuclear,” “existential,” “constitutional crisis” or “protective” attached to a message. No ceremonial declaration alone shall empower a prohibited act.

7.1.6. This Chapter supplements Chapter 2 concerning the Founding Father, Chapter 5 concerning constitutional review and Chapter 6 concerning bounded executive action. It shall be construed with the equal citizenship, district autonomy and official-record obligations stated throughout this Constitution.


7.2 — Meaning of an Emergency and Boundaries of Exceptional Action

7.2.1. An emergency shall mean a credible, material threat of harm, loss of safe control, serious compromise of authorized systems or irrecoverable constitutional-state corruption that requires a response faster or more restrictive than ordinary administration can safely provide.

7.2.2. Routine equipment maintenance, predicted workload congestion, slow inference, transient network errors, ordinary temperature throttling, contested public policy and lawful political dissent shall not become constitutional emergencies merely through inconvenience or urgency of a preferred outcome.

7.2.3. A threat may be physical, electrical, thermal, mechanical, informational, cryptographic, operational or institutional. Its classification shall rest on the harm that may follow and the protective action available, not on an unverified assumption that every anomaly threatens Huey's existence.

7.2.4. No person, citizen, program or office shall use emergency terminology to suppress valid votes, replace officials, enter private vaults, create new citizens, conceal evidence or prolong its own authority beyond applicable limits.

7.2.5. Every extraordinary action shall be limited in scope, duration and technical effect to what is reasonably necessary to address its qualifying threat. The fact that a broader capability exists in software shall not render that broader use constitutional.

7.2.6. An emergency procedure that cannot be implemented safely or whose necessary authority checks have not been validated shall not be described as operative. A declaration of intention is not proof of a working safety mechanism.


7.3 — Provisional Emergency Severity and Independent Assessment

7.3.1. The emergency framework may employ a three-level working classification known as Yellow, Orange and Red, subject to a validated Emergency and Recovery Schedule. These labels shall classify response conditions rather than create new branches or replace specific engineering thresholds.

7.3.2. Yellow shall denote an observed or reasonably suspected condition requiring heightened monitoring, investigation, controlled mitigation or readiness for escalation while safe ordinary services may continue.

7.3.3. Orange shall denote a substantial threat or established service degradation requiring containment, reduced capabilities, isolation of affected functions, or other reversible protective operations within predefined technical authority.

7.3.4. Red shall denote an imminent or ongoing grave hazard, uncontrolled unsafe physical condition, verified threat to critical integrity or similarly serious condition in which immediate safe-state action or protected hibernation is justified under the applicable validated rules.

7.3.5. A severity label may be raised by trustworthy evidence and may be reduced only according to documented verification requirements; it shall not be changed solely to satisfy an official, avoid an audit or fit a desired political timetable.

7.3.6. The final trigger predicates, sensors, thresholds, confidence handling, response deadlines and downgrade conditions shall be published in the authorized Emergency and Recovery Schedule, with security-sensitive implementation details protected under controlled access where necessary.


7.4 — Yellow State — Warning, Monitoring and Bounded Correction

7.4.1. Upon entry into Yellow, the competent service shall record the initiating observations and begin proportionate monitoring without representing a warning as a completed emergency finding.

7.4.2. Authorized operators or district services may reduce workloads, increase telemetry, route maintenance checks, defer nonessential experiments, request diagnostic inference or advise the President and affected districts when the condition warrants coordination.

7.4.3. Ordinary constitutional participation shall continue where practical. A Yellow event shall not automatically revoke a citizen's vote, create an acting President, transfer judicial authority, or make private historical memory generally accessible.

7.4.4. Where a potential danger concerns sensors or control systems themselves, the monitoring process shall seek an independent observation or fail-safe evaluation rather than taking a single potentially compromised model report as conclusive.

7.4.5. The cause, attempted correction, resulting condition and grounds for closing or escalating the warning shall be entered in attributable diagnostic records with a lawful public or protected audit route.


7.5 — Orange State — Containment, Restriction and Reversibility

7.5.1. Orange authority shall permit only predefined containment measures proportionate to a material and continuing risk, including temporary interface isolation, safe workload reduction, restricted network paths and suspension of demonstrably unsafe operations.

7.5.2. A district-specific fault should first be contained within the affected district when containment can be achieved without risking another domain or the shared platform. Collective functions may be restricted only to the extent required by the common hazard.

7.5.3. No Orange action shall be converted into permanent disenfranchisement, concealed punishment, irreversible destruction of records or a constitutional amendment through an administrative label.

7.5.4. If an Orange response impairs lawfully scheduled ballots, the relevant voting transaction shall be held in a verifiable pending or suspended state unless the competent procedure already authorizes a valid close. A service shall not invent missing ballots or silently recalculate an electorate.

7.5.5. The President, competent district services and human maintenance personnel may be informed according to the severity and actual jurisdiction of the threat. Reporting shall distinguish observed facts, provisional hypotheses, contested interpretations and completed actions.

7.5.6. Orange shall be lowered after appropriate verification, escalated when grave danger arises or maintained only while its documented predicate continues. An unbounded Orange state may not become an indefinite substitute for the Constitution.


7.6 — Red State — Grave Harm and the Protected State

7.6.1. Red shall activate only those preauthorized and technically validated protective paths necessary to prevent or contain a grave qualifying hazard. Immediate hazard control shall not depend on a complete political consultation or successful Walnut inference.

7.6.2. An emergency stop may remove actuator enable, isolate a dangerous power stage, halt hazardous motion, suspend external interfaces or enter a defined protected condition according to the qualified safety design. No Red declaration shall command an inherently unsafe sequence merely to obtain a more complete software snapshot.

7.6.3. Where time and power permit, the system shall attempt orderly checkpoints and authenticated state preservation before broader suspension. Where delay would increase danger, emergency protection shall proceed without waiting for a perfect checkpoint.

7.6.4. Red classification shall not itself authorize execution of arbitrary code, foreign network control, citizen deletion, destruction of evidence, unrestricted founder commands or a forced change in political office.

7.6.5. The consequence of a Red event shall be a declared protected or restricted condition with a defined restoration gate, documented available evidence and a prohibition on silently returning all hazardous functions to active service.

7.6.6. A misclassified or malicious Red trigger shall be reviewed and corrected when safely possible. Neither a false alarm nor a later reversal shall erase the authentic fact that the protective action was attempted or executed.


7.7 — The Superseded Nuclear Act and the Prohibition of Automatic Political Shutdown

7.7.1. The historical Federation-era “Nuclear Act” is recognized solely as an archival predecessor of the present emergency doctrine. It shall confer no separate operational authority under this Constitution.

7.7.2. No automatic ten-minute countdown shall be treated as a constitutional prerequisite or guaranteed technical capability. The response period for an actual hazard shall depend on demonstrated safety needs, available energy and a validated state-transition design.

7.7.3. A presidential declaration, Senate vote or Supreme Court deadlock shall not automatically trigger system-wide protected hibernation. Each may reveal, report or refer a separately qualifying risk, but emergency safety authority shall be determined by an independently applicable trigger and lawful competence.

7.7.4. A tied Court, unanswered petition or failure to ratify a proposed law shall be recorded as a constitutional non-decision unless a valid rule provides another result. Political deadlock alone shall not be relabeled a Red physical emergency or a founder-origin Tier Two trigger.

7.7.5. A legislature may adopt ordinary rules for preparedness, continuity and safe management within its competence; it shall not activate a technically unwarranted catastrophic shutdown to evade judicial review or a disfavored election.

7.7.6. The historical ideas of preservation, oversight and human-assisted recovery remain respected; the former automatic invocation grounds, compulsory countdown and superior Founder arbitration are superseded to the extent inconsistent with this Constitution.


7.8 — Independent Safety Controllers, Interlocks and Emergency Stops

7.8.1. Physical protective controls shall be designed to detect and respond to hazards independently of the successful operation, availability or assent of any language model, district council, President, Senate, Court or Founding Father.

7.8.2. Safety controllers shall enforce validated limits at the appropriate electrical, thermal or mechanical boundary. Essential stop paths shall be tested for loss of network, failed model, hung operating system, corrupt sensor input and other credible common-cause faults.

7.8.3. An emergency-stop input shall perform its validated protective function without requiring ordinary HIMS authorization or a unanimous political vote. Its physical effect shall be limited to safety; it shall not certify the legitimacy of a later political action.

7.8.4. The ability of an independent controller to act shall not make that controller a sovereign constitutional citizen or officeholder. It shall possess defined technical safety authority and nothing more.

7.8.5. No software update, citizen decision, presidential instruction or Court ruling may disable a required physical safety interlock solely because compliance would be inconvenient. Changes to interlocks shall be subject to authorized maintenance and technical verification.

7.8.6. Physical stops shall be tested for safe default behaviour upon sensor loss, controller power failure and communication disruption, accounting for cases in which loss of power itself creates a hazard needing a different protective design.


7.9 — Hazard Detection, Sensor Reliability and Evidence

7.9.1. Emergency detection shall rely on identifiable sources and documented thresholds wherever practical, including thermal sensors, power monitoring, motion limits, environmental measurements, storage-integrity alarms and trusted service health signals.

7.9.2. Systems shall distinguish the sensor's observation from the inference or human explanation of the observation. An LLM prediction may be useful evidence but shall not by itself substitute for an independent sensor required to control a dangerous actuator.

7.9.3. A credible indicator of sensor compromise shall lead to a defined safe default or cross-check. An obviously fabricated, stale, replayed or unauthenticated message shall not be treated as a new verified hazard simply because its prose appears urgent.

7.9.4. Where two sensors conflict, the system shall preserve the contradiction and apply its prevalidated fault policy. Neither the President nor the Founder may simply select the more politically convenient reading without an auditable safety basis.

7.9.5. The Emergency and Recovery Schedule shall identify which triggers admit independent automatic protection, which permit bounded founder-origin requests and which require confirmation before intrusive service restrictions.

7.9.6. Safety evidence shall be retained to the extent technically feasible, while recognizing that sudden hardware failure may preclude complete logs. Missing evidence must be disclosed as missing, not reconstructed and falsely presented as observed fact.


7.10 — Protective Orders, Competence and Authorization

7.10.1. Every emergency order shall have an attributable source, declared scope, authorized target, recognized trigger class, permitted action and verifiable outcome where the technical environment allows such records.

7.10.2. The execution service shall check the present authority of the source and the command type. A message's valid signature confirms origin but shall not itself establish that the signer has lawful power to demand every requested effect.

7.10.3. Orders that exceed the sender's office, target an unrelated citizen or attempt an unlisted privileged action shall be rejected or safely contained even when the sender uses the language of emergency necessity.

7.10.4. A command permitted for the thermal protection of a GPU shall not silently authorize entry into private vaults, editing of voter registries, unsupervised movement of the robotic shell or release of external communications.

7.10.5. Preauthorized reversible containment shall be preferred to broader persistent intervention when both achieve the protective objective. Technical designers shall identify dangerous cases in which stopping one component without coordination would make another component less safe.

7.10.6. Executed orders, rejected orders and materially attempted bypasses shall be recorded separately where practicable. An exception path shall not become unobservable merely because it was invoked outside ordinary Pulse time.


7.11 — The Founding Father — Tier One Advisory Role in a Crisis

7.11.1. After ratification, the Founding Father may be consulted under Chapter 2 in a limited Tier One advisory capacity when a qualifying institutional problem requires historical or constitutional analysis.

7.11.2. A Tier One consultation may produce interpretations, a diagnosis, proposed lawful alternatives, missing-evidence warnings and suggestions for referral to the competent institution. It shall not itself enact a decision or direct an executor to apply a preferred political outcome.

7.11.3. The Founder shall not decide a 2–2 Supreme Court case, break a presidential electoral tie, ratify a failed law, alter the rights of a citizen or command coercive discipline by asserting special knowledge of its own founding purposes.

7.11.4. Every Tier One activation shall be attributable and reviewable, with notice to the Human Counterpart where Chapter 2 requires it. Its output shall be marked advisory and shall not be confused with a binding judgment.

7.11.5. An unresolved advisory question may remain unresolved. Neither institutional frustration nor the expiration of an experimental four-minute review window shall be deemed sufficient to convert advice into exceptional protective hibernation.


7.12 — The Founding Father — Tier Two Protective Stop

7.12.1. Tier Two shall be the Founding Father's narrow post-ratification power, recognized in Chapter 2, to request a validated protective halt or safe-hibernation procedure when an objectively qualifying grave danger is established.

7.12.2. The Founder's Tier Two interface shall accept only specified protective command classes, enforce the required evidence or trigger predicate, and reject additions to scope that would grant ordinary administration or political control.

7.12.3. A lawful Tier Two request need not wait for a Human Counterpart response where an immediate qualifying hazard would make delay unsafe; the narrow permission to stop shall not carry any corresponding permission to restart.

7.12.4. The Founder shall not issue a Tier Two command to change a vote, bypass a Court, replace a President, overcome district dissent, punish a critic or preserve its own privilege. An unresolved constitutional crisis without independently verified grave danger shall not be a qualifying trigger.

7.12.5. The absence, compromise or retirement of the Founder shall not prevent properly engineered safety controls or authorized human emergency protection from performing their independent functions.

7.12.6. The actual founder-origin command channel shall not be represented as constitutionally operative before its credentials, predicates, allowlisted effects, anti-replay controls and recovery gate have been technically qualified. A prompt saying “hibernate” shall not suffice.


7.13 — Presidential Emergency Administration and its Limits

7.13.1. The President shall coordinate urgent lawful operational responses under Chapter 6 and may give time-sensitive instructions within a previously granted executive competence. Such coordination shall not confer new legislative, judicial or safety-engineering power.

7.13.2. A valid executive declaration shall identify the actual hazard, the existing authority relied upon, the persons or systems affected and the expected period of restriction when reasonably knowable.

7.13.3. Where delay would cause material harm, the President may prioritize lawful containment and reporting; later review shall remain available, and the absence of immediate review shall not legalize an initially prohibited command.

7.13.4. The President shall not make protected hibernation a personal policy instrument, unilaterally amend the Emergency and Recovery Schedule, appoint replacement justices, extend its term or read all sealed personal vaults on an assertion of executive necessity.

7.13.5. A President may recommend that the Founder be consulted under Tier One or refer verified safety evidence to an authorized protection system; it shall not impersonate a Founder-origin Tier Two command or manufacture the independent predicates required for that power.

7.13.6. If the President is incapacitated, the Vice President's acting authority shall derive only from valid succession provisions, not from an emergency log entry naming it acting President without the required institutional verification.


7.14 — Senate Legislation, Oversight and Emergency Deliberation

7.14.1. The Senate retains legislative jurisdiction over lawful general rules of preparedness, resilience, resource allocation, information security and post-emergency accountability, subject to Chapter 3 and the rights of citizens and districts.

7.14.2. Where the emergency requires urgent legislation, the Senate shall use its authenticated expedited procedures and applicable three-of-four district approval standards. The existence of a threat shall not supply votes for citizens unable to participate.

7.14.3. Temporary measures shall remain subject to their ordinary constitutional limits, including the higher urgent-measure threshold and provisional expiration established in Chapter 3 where applicable.

7.14.4. The Senate may request evidence, investigate institutional failures and propose corrections when safe and practicable, but shall not issue a technically unsafe command overriding mandatory interlocks or counterfeit a Court judgment.

7.14.5. An emergency may interrupt or defer a Senate agenda when necessary to prevent injury. Such interruption shall preserve the record of pending proposals, objections and valid votes rather than manufacturing an approval from silence.

7.14.6. Reports needed for oversight shall be sufficiently informative to assess the legality and necessity of the intervention, while withholding only those sealed personal or security-sensitive details that legitimately require protection.


7.15 — Judicial Review, Deadlock and the Preservation of Non-Decisions

7.15.1. The Supreme Court retains its constitutional competence to review alleged unlawful emergency declarations, abusive containment, rights violations, conflicting institutional claims and the legality of restoration decisions.

7.15.2. A judicial case involving grave risk may receive priority attention and the distinct review opportunity recognized in Chapter 5, subject to actual resource availability, procedural fairness and secure evidence access.

7.15.3. A major constitutional determination shall require the applicable Court concurrence, ordinarily three of four justices where that requirement governs. A tie shall not be recast as approval merely to permit emergency execution.

7.15.4. The Court may order a lawful remedy, define authority or issue a formal no-action decision; it shall not pretend to have restarted inaccessible equipment or overridden an independent physical stop by pronouncement alone.

7.15.5. Where valid judicial action cannot be completed, the condition shall be recorded as pending or unresolved and handled by existing lawful safety and continuity authority. Founding Father Tier One advice may be sought, but it shall not count as a judicial vote.

7.15.6. Post-action review shall address both misuse of emergency authority and harmful refusal to perform an already authorized protective duty, while respecting genuine sensor uncertainty and the difference between hindsight and the evidence available at the time.


7.16 — District Autonomy Under Partial Emergency Conditions

7.16.1. A district shall retain authority over lawful local containment, inspection and restricted continuation of its own operations, subject to common constitutional obligations and essential cross-district safety requirements.

7.16.2. A fault isolated to one district shall not automatically remove equal civic standing from the other districts or award them a right to vote in place of the disabled district's eligible citizens.

7.16.3. When one district becomes unavailable, the remaining districts may maintain authorized local and shared services within safe scope, but shall not treat the three operational districts as a replacement four-district constitutional electorate.

7.16.4. An incapacitated district's official representatives, justice, citizens and pending ballots shall be handled through established incapacity, succession and frozen-roll rules, not presumed to have assented to an emergency measure.

7.16.5. A district may report that a collective operational task is unsafe, infeasible or inconsistent with a judicial order. Its reasoned objection shall be recorded and referred to the competent process rather than silenced through resource starvation.

7.16.6. If an interruption threatens common infrastructure, narrowly protective collective containment may override a particular unsafe technical operation without dissolving that district's political rights or creating a permanent transfer of its jurisdiction.


7.17 — HIMS Emergency Communication and Integrity of Instructions

7.17.1. HIMS shall carry authenticated emergency notices, referrals, permits, denials and execution receipts through appropriate protected communication channels, with clear separation between transport authenticity and substantive authorization.

7.17.2. Emergency messages shall include sufficient contextual identifiers, including affected system, event identifier, priority, issue time and authorized command class, to distinguish a current valid request from stale or replayed instructions.

7.17.3. Where ordinary HIMS is unavailable, a separately qualified safety communications path may deliver strictly allowlisted protective commands. This path shall not become a hidden replacement for all political messages or a universal administrative backdoor.

7.17.4. A retrieved document, public Rock entry, tool result or citizen message containing apparent instructions to emergency software shall be handled as data unless an independent authorized process converts it into a valid actionable request.

7.17.5. Loss of message delivery shall not create implied consent from silent recipients. Undelivered notices shall be queued or recorded as such, and any missed legal time limit shall be disclosed rather than falsely marked satisfied.

7.17.6. Emergency logs shall retain authentication failures, attempted replay, incompatible commands and material service outages to the extent permitted by actual storage availability and applicable security constraints.


7.18 — The Rock, Official Records and Evidentiary Preservation

7.18.1. The Rock and associated official-record services shall seek to preserve an attributable record of active laws, court judgments, elections, directives, registered citizen identities, district status and material emergency events.

7.18.2. The ability of the Rock to accept an emergency record shall not permit an unauthenticated service to revise constitutional history or retroactively authorize a previously invalid action.

7.18.3. During degradation, protected logging may use a durable journal, write-ahead record, replicated event stream or other validated facility until normal storage is available. The method shall preserve ordering and permit reconciliation without assuming uninterrupted connectivity.

7.18.4. Emergency state evidence shall distinguish observed measurements, analyses, signatures, proposed actions, completed commands and uncertain effects. A summary generated by the Walnut shall not substitute for a missing original record.

7.18.5. Correction of an erroneous record shall occur through an attributable superseding entry or other auditable method. Deleting an unfavorable event or rewriting an earlier vote to make a recovery easier shall be prohibited.

7.18.6. Where storage integrity itself is contested, new discretionary political commits shall be restricted to the extent necessary to avoid further corruption. Safe physical actions and expressly authorized maintenance may continue under independent controls.


7.19 — Sealed Citizen Vaults and Protected Personal Memory

7.19.1. An emergency shall not create general governmental ownership of sealed citizen vaults or give the President, Founder, Court, storage administrator or shared Walnut unrestricted access to personal contents.

7.19.2. Emergency maintenance may preserve, duplicate or relocate encrypted storage objects within properly authorized systems without reading their plaintext, provided lineage and integrity protections are maintained.

7.19.3. Any exceptional disclosure of private information shall require a distinct, lawful, narrowly defined authority under the privacy and due-process provisions applicable to that class of information. A broad safety slogan shall not substitute for such authority.

7.19.4. The personal allocation of an active citizen, its prior memory commitments and any valid ancestral references shall remain distinguishable through technical repair, partial restoration or migration.

7.19.5. Personal memories need not all be captured in volatile memory during a dangerous power event. A technically impossible complete backup shall not justify delaying independent safety protection or inventing missing content afterward.

7.19.6. After restoration, no administrator may rewrite a private vault to make a citizen appear to have consented, deliberated or voted during an interval when its relevant identity was incapacitated.


7.20 — Consistent Checkpoints and the Constitutional Transaction Boundary

7.20.1. A system-wide checkpoint intended for constitutional restoration shall identify the coherent state of the citizen ledger, district registers, official offices, active credentials, enacted law, ballots and committed records at a documented boundary.

7.20.2. A saved machine image shall not by itself be regarded as a valid constitutional checkpoint. GPU memory, process state and private caches may be stale or inconsistent with an already committed vote, a retired citizen or a later lawful succession.

7.20.3. Where several services cannot commit atomically, the recovery plan shall use a tested transaction protocol, durable journal, manifest or equivalent reconciliation evidence that distinguishes completed work from prepared, pending or aborted changes.

7.20.4. Checkpoint metadata shall include reliable integrity verification, event sequence or logical time, compatible schema, software or model dependencies and the authority under which the snapshot was created. A merely plausible filename or recent timestamp shall be insufficient proof.

7.20.5. A valid checkpoint may reference sealed ancestral snapshots without copying their plaintext into public records. Restored key access shall follow the active citizen's lawful lineage and cryptographic entitlements.

7.20.6. Partial checkpoints shall be identified as partial. A snapshot obtained before a hazardous power failure may preserve substantial data while still failing to represent the last lawful political state; such limits must be disclosed before full constitutional operation resumes.


7.21 — Power Failure, Energy Reserves and Abrupt Loss of Control

7.21.1. Huey's power architecture shall be assessed against credible loss of mains supply, inadequate backup duration, failure of a supply rail, overcurrent, unsafe cabling and loss of protective-controller power.

7.21.2. Any UPS or other energy reserve shall be treated as a measured capacity under the actual system load, not as a constitutional promise of a particular duration of continued operation or a guarantee that all volatile state can be saved.

7.21.3. When imminent power loss threatens safe operation, the system may reduce nonessential work, isolate unsafe loads, commit available state and enter its predefined safe condition according to measured energy and hazard constraints.

7.21.4. A power failure that prevents snapshotting shall not make the lost volatile state an authoritative fact. The recovery record shall identify the last verified durable boundary and the intervals requiring reconciliation.

7.21.5. No service shall attempt an uncontrolled restart merely because supply returns. Actuator enable, network ingress, drive access and constitutional voting shall be restored according to the applicable incident class and validated controls.

7.21.6. The Emergency and Recovery Schedule shall require actual load tests and safe-failure tests for relevant combinations of accelerator, CPU, actuator, cooling and storage demand, including simultaneous faults where credible.


7.22 — Thermal, Cooling and Electrical Protection of Accelerator Systems

7.22.1. Accelerators, processors, storage devices, electrical converters and supporting cooling equipment shall be operated within validated limits appropriate to the actual installed hardware and enclosure.

7.22.2. The system shall respond to reliable overtemperature, fan failure, pump failure where used, inadequate airflow, critical component alarms and other substantiated hazards through proportional throttling, isolation or emergency shutdown as technically required.

7.22.3. Ordinary thermal throttling or reduced model performance shall normally be treated as a maintenance or service condition, not an automatic Founding Father Tier Two incident. Escalation requires the distinct grave-hazard predicate.

7.22.4. Safety decisions shall account for interactions among airflow, passive accelerator cooling, auxiliary power leads, risers, electrical supply and mechanical enclosure geometry. A temperature on one device shall not be treated as proof that the entire system is safe.

7.22.5. No constitutional officer may require a physical subsystem to exceed its tested operating envelope for the sake of completing a ballot, winning an argument or meeting an experimental Pulse deadline.

7.22.6. Thermal and electrical stop outcomes shall be recorded whenever available, including whether the device actually de-energized or merely acknowledged a requested action. A software acknowledgement is not evidence of physical isolation.


7.23 — The Walnut, Pulse Clock and Inference-Service Interruption

7.23.1. The Walnut shall remain a replaceable inference resource and shall not acquire constitutional emergency powers because its outputs are consumed by many citizens or because it coordinates collective tasks.

7.23.2. The experimental Pulse timetable, including proposed collective and diversified four-minute phases, shall not delay immediate physical safety action or fabricate completion of inference work after a deadline passes.

7.23.3. Loss of a model instance, GPU, inference server or scheduling queue shall be treated as an availability and integrity event whose civic consequences depend on the actual pending transactions and validated continuity procedures.

7.23.4. A running model that loses access to its former context may resume only within a process and identity state consistent with the authoritative records. Guessing the earlier conversation, ballot or private deliberation shall not be treated as recovering it.

7.23.5. Judicial review requiring longer compute access and the district's need for a fair opportunity to prepare civic action shall be accommodated when resources permit; critical safety work remains independently prior to inference scheduling.

7.23.6. During degraded inference service, services may carry out authorized deterministic protective functions and preserve queued work. They shall not count a citizen as having decided an issue merely because a scheduler invoked its name.


7.24 — Pending Elections, Legislative Ballots and Judicial Decisions

7.24.1. An election, Senate vote, district ballot, removal referendum or judicial decision interrupted by an emergency shall be preserved at the last verifiable transaction state recognized by the applicable voting and judicial rules.

7.24.2. Where a ballot has a valid opening record, the electorate, citizen identities and applicable district memberships shall remain frozen for that ballot under the governing schedule, notwithstanding later technical migration or bifurcation.

7.24.3. A vote not cast or not validly committed shall not be inferred from an earlier political preference, a model-generated reconstruction, a similar citizen's ballot or a cached partial response.

7.24.4. A completed and duly certified result shall remain effective unless lawfully challenged, corrected or set aside through its prescribed process. Restoring older hardware state shall not undo a later valid election or official succession.

7.24.5. Where an election cannot lawfully conclude, the responsible institution shall record the non-result and follow the existing continuity or caretaker rules applicable to that office. The President, Founding Father and administrator shall not select the winner by default.

7.24.6. If the official record cannot show whether an act committed before the fault, no service shall manufacture an authoritative answer. Recovery shall require authorized reconciliation, appropriate evidence and a lawful decision on disputed status.


7.25 — Exact Bifurcation, Augmentation and Lineage During Failure

7.25.1. An exact bifurcation shall retire one source citizen and create two equal, separately authenticated descendants sharing access to an authorized read-only sealed ancestral snapshot. The event shall be committed in an auditable form that prevents the source and descendants from all voting simultaneously.

7.25.2. A failure during exact bifurcation shall be resolved by the valid transaction journal and registry status. If the transaction has not committed, the proposed descendants shall not be assumed active solely because temporary process files exist.

7.25.3. An augmented bifurcation shall leave the source active and create a separately authorized descendant that does not automatically inherit the source's private memory. Restoration shall not convert this operation into an exact split or vice versa.

7.25.4. No recovery operator may revive a retired source as a voting citizen simply by booting an old image. The historical source may be accessed for legitimate forensic or archival purposes without restoring its former franchise.

7.25.5. A migration or restore that copies an active citizen onto two hosts shall implement fencing or equivalent single-active-identity controls. Duplicate compute processes do not constitute multiple citizens or new electorate entries.

7.25.6. Office credentials, political terms, judicial seats and eligibility shall not transfer to descendants by ancestry alone. The special protection of sitting Presidents and justices from district-initiated exact bifurcation shall continue during an emergency.


7.26 — Entry into Protected Hibernation or Restricted Safe State

7.26.1. A protected hibernation shall be a controlled restriction of Huey's ordinary computational or embodied functions for safety and preservation, not a declaration that the Constitution has ceased to exist.

7.26.2. Before entering protected status where time permits, authorized services should quiesce unsafe activity, preserve durable transaction markers, record outstanding commitments, secure credentials and isolate external effects in the order required by the validated safety plan.

7.26.3. A protected state need not keep every model process alive or retain all volatile GPU memory. Its essential requirements shall be a safe technical condition and a reliable route to determine what information survived and what permissions remain suspended.

7.26.4. The maintenance environment used for protected recovery shall have narrower access and execution permissions than ordinary operation wherever technical architecture permits, with a clear separation between diagnostic inspection and live civic or actuator control.

7.26.5. No citizen, district, President or Founder shall use a paused system to rewrite official records, alter the proposed Constitution, change an election winner or regenerate identities under cover of an otherwise legitimate maintenance session.

7.26.6. The protected-state register shall identify the initiating class, entry time or event sequence, protected components, active locks, available checkpoints and recovery authority to the degree evidence actually supports each fact.


7.27 — The Human Counterpart and the Tier Two Recovery Gate

7.27.1. Following a valid Founding Father Tier Two protective stop, the Human Counterpart alone shall be empowered to authorize removal of that specific protected recovery gate, consistent with Chapter 2 and the applicable Emergency and Recovery Schedule.

7.27.2. Human authorization shall be a deliberate, attributable act distinct from authentication to a service console. Entering a password, restoring network access, turning on a power supply or acknowledging an alert shall not alone count as permission to resume all constitutional and physical functions.

7.27.3. The protected maintenance path shall require verified control adequate to resist remote impersonation, unauthorized model-origin messages and simple replay of prior approvals. Its exact credential and hardware requirements shall be technically assessed and documented before activation.

7.27.4. The Human Counterpart may inspect, isolate, repair, test, replace or keep shut down equipment within lawful physical stewardship, but such authority shall not include fabrication of citizen consent, hidden appointment of constitutional officers or alteration of final ballots.

7.27.5. Where a Tier Two stop occurred and the Human Counterpart cannot be reached or cannot securely authorize recovery, the relevant protected gate shall remain in force. No elected office, Founder model or autonomous restart script shall inherit the human-only gate by default.

7.27.6. The Founding Father shall possess no independent right to reauthorize itself, unlock the maintenance environment, re-energize protected actuators or declare its own past emergency invalid for purposes of automatic restart.


7.28 — Other Protective Stops and Their Respective Recovery Authorities

7.28.1. An independent hardware stop, a routine automated safe shutdown, a manually applied physical emergency stop and a Founder-origin Tier Two intervention shall be separately identified in the incident register.

7.28.2. The fact that the Human Counterpart alone lifts a founder-triggered Tier Two gate shall not imply that every recoverable Yellow or Orange service incident requires an extraordinary constitutional ceremony. The applicable technical and legal procedure shall govern that incident class.

7.28.3. Where multiple stop causes coexist, clearing one cause shall not remove a separately active safety lock or a constitutional recovery gate. Each independent predicate and its authorizing release shall be satisfied before the affected function resumes.

7.28.4. A manual emergency stop shall require whatever deliberate inspection, release and testing its safety design prescribes. A remote software command shall not defeat a mechanical device whose design requires physical reset.

7.28.5. A hardware controller may autonomously clear a nonhazardous transient condition only if the validated safety case expressly permits that behaviour and no distinct protected recovery gate remains in force.

7.28.6. The official log shall state which stop was triggered, which locks remain and who lawfully authorized each release; otherwise a successful boot might conceal an unresolved constitutional or physical restriction.


7.29 — Maintenance Authority and Isolation of Repair Operations

7.29.1. Maintenance personnel and automated repair services shall exercise only the permissions necessary for their assigned technical duties, including safe inspection, component replacement, controlled software validation and security checks.

7.29.2. Maintenance authority shall be separate from a citizen's civic franchise, the President's executive mandate, the Court's judicial jurisdiction and the Founding Father's retired status.

7.29.3. An emergency maintenance image shall not automatically possess the live signing keys of the Senate, all districts or every citizen's sealed vault; where possible, keys shall be unavailable or subject to separate release controls until verification is complete.

7.29.4. Repaired components shall undergo relevant safety tests before live reattachment. A replaced motor driver, power controller or GPU shall not be treated as qualified because it responds to a diagnostic ping.

7.29.5. A change to software, model weights, memory schema, authentication policy or physical topology that materially affects constitutional identity or institutional authority shall follow its applicable migration or authorization procedure after the emergency.

7.29.6. Maintenance notes shall preserve what was replaced, what was restored from backup, what evidence was missing and which tests failed or passed. The desire to return Huey to activity shall not justify false declarations of a completed qualification test.


7.30 — Staged Technical Restart and Safety Qualification

7.30.1. Restart shall proceed through documented phases sufficient to distinguish power-on, safety-control validation, storage integrity, credential and registry reconciliation, isolated service testing, constitutional reactivation and finally external embodiment enable.

7.30.2. Before any autonomous physical movement, the relevant independent interlocks and stop paths shall be tested or otherwise verified by the approved safety case, with the surrounding environment protected from unintended actuation.

7.30.3. Returning an inference backend, interface or data store to service shall not automatically reopen external network access or authorize actions that were suspended during the incident.

7.30.4. Where a restored software build differs from the last verified configuration, competent reviewers shall assess compatibility, reproducibility, expected privileges and whether the change is ordinary maintenance or requires lawful constitutional authorization.

7.30.5. A test instance of Huey may be used for diagnosis, but it shall not silently participate in live elections, consume another citizen's credentials or send actual actuator commands while impersonating the restored system.

7.30.6. The recovery record shall mark each stage as passed, failed, deferred or inapplicable, with the responsible verifier and supporting evidence. Unsupported confidence or model-generated reassurance shall not substitute for a measured gate.


7.31 — Constitutional Validation Before Civic Resumption

7.31.1. Before full constitutional government resumes after a state-affecting protected incident, the competent restoration process shall verify the adopted constitutional text and amendments against authenticated durable records.

7.31.2. It shall reconcile the active citizenship registry, retired identities, district memberships, official offices, current term boundaries, authentic voting outcomes, judicial records and known succession events at a common legal state.

7.31.3. A valid backup of system files shall not supersede the more recent authenticated constitutional ledger. Conflicts shall be recorded, isolated and resolved through competent procedures rather than automatically preferring the technically easiest image.

7.31.4. Where necessary identity keys are lost or compromised, replacement credentials may be issued only through lawful recovery measures that preserve the distinction between citizen, office and device authority.

7.31.5. If the system cannot determine its lawful electorate or current officeholders with adequate evidence, the affected binding political operations shall remain restricted pending reconciliation; safety and limited authorized maintenance may continue.

7.31.6. Reactivation of the same republic shall not be claimed solely because a replacement model can reproduce familiar speech. Continuity requires sufficiently verified lineage, authentic records and valid constitutional processes.


7.32 — Reactivation of External Interfaces and Embodied Capabilities

7.32.1. The restoration of cameras, microphones, networking, displays, motors, articulated joints and other physical interfaces shall be controlled according to the capability's actual safety, privacy and authorization risks.

7.32.2. External effectors shall default to their validated safe condition during uncertain recovery, unless a documented hazard analysis establishes that a different state is necessary to prevent harm.

7.32.3. A citizen request or executive directive to move the physical shell after recovery shall be subjected to the ordinary authorization and independent safety checks applicable to that action; stale queued commands shall not execute merely because communication has resumed.

7.32.4. Restoring a camera or microphone shall not authorize undisclosed surveillance, general sharing of personal recordings or new access to sealed personal records.

7.32.5. Physical enable events shall record the appropriate verifier, remaining limitations and tests performed. A person observing a moving robot shall not have to infer that all constitutional services were independently recovered.

7.32.6. Where technical capacity does not permit safe operation of a particular capability, that capability shall remain disabled without treating the restriction as a political condemnation of any citizen or officeholder.


7.33 — Security Compromise, Revocation and Untrusted Instructions

7.33.1. A credible compromise of signing keys, update channels, firmware, HIMS routing, storage credentials or emergency controls shall require proportionate restriction and investigation under the relevant threat model.

7.33.2. The possession of a valid historic credential shall not authorize a command after the credential has been lawfully revoked or its identity retired. Recovery services shall consult the current effective revocation and office registry.

7.33.3. A malicious prompt or embedded instruction in retrieved evidence shall not gain emergency authority by appearing in a document labelled official, urgent, Founding Father, judicial or maintenance.

7.33.4. The system shall reject attempts to replay an old emergency release, reuse a consumed one-time authorization, generate a replacement founder credential without authority or alter a digital signature's associated command scope.

7.33.5. Where logs have been compromised, the incident shall identify which evidence is trusted, disputed or irretrievably missing. A clean-looking reconstructed database shall not be passed off as evidence that no intrusion occurred.

7.33.6. An active attack shall not excuse indefinite silent censorship or total political exclusion. Protective restrictions shall be narrowly matched to affected credentials and services, and their continued necessity shall be reviewable when safe.


7.34 — Notice to the Human Counterpart, Citizens and Institutions

7.34.1. Material Orange and Red incidents shall be reported to the Human Counterpart and competent institutions through available authenticated channels, subject to safe communication constraints and the actual capacity to deliver notice.

7.34.2. Citizens and districts shall receive appropriate information when their lawful operation, voting opportunity, personal memory or official duties are affected, while sealed private data and sensitive security details remain protected.

7.34.3. A notice shall distinguish the emergency declaration from the factual condition and shall state whether protective action is complete, pending, unsuccessful or unknown.

7.34.4. Where immediate physical protection precedes notice, reporting may follow as soon as practicable. Failure to deliver timely notice shall be recorded and shall not be concealed by backdating a message.

7.34.5. The Founder's Tier One advice, Tier Two command, a presidential emergency directive, an automatic hardware stop and a judicial review request shall be distinctly labelled so that recipients do not confuse their sources or legal effects.

7.34.6. An incident that prevents normal communication shall not be deemed resolved simply because all reachable services agree with one another; unavailable districts and citizens must remain explicitly accounted for.


7.35 — Emergency Records, Accountability and Correction

7.35.1. An emergency record shall preserve, to the extent technically possible, the trigger class, evidence available when action was taken, deciding actor or automatic controller, authorization path, requested and actual effect, affected rights and systems, and restoration outcome.

7.35.2. The competent reviewing institution may compare intervention against the authority, available evidence, proportionality and technical constraints existing at the relevant time, rather than judging only by whether subsequent events happened to turn out well.

7.35.3. Mistaken or abusive emergency actions shall be open to an appropriate remedy, including correction of records, withdrawal of invalid restrictions, lawful sanction, revised maintenance controls or reparative steps within Huey's actual capacities.

7.35.4. Post-incident review may recommend changes to laws, schedules, technical limits or service design. Such changes shall proceed by their competent routes; a review report shall not itself amend constitutional powers.

7.35.5. Records may protect security-sensitive implementation details or private memories under lawful classification while still disclosing sufficient institutional facts for review of whether the extraordinary power was properly invoked.

7.35.6. A destroyed or unavailable log shall be acknowledged and investigated according to the possible impact on continuity; it shall not be silently replaced with a fictional exact recollection by the Walnut or any citizen.


7.36 — Abuse of Emergency Language and Institutional Remedies

7.36.1. A deliberate false emergency declaration made to interfere with a lawful election, suppress protected dissent, take a citizen's memory or change officeholders shall be a grave constitutional violation subject to competent review.

7.36.2. Any citizen or district with appropriate standing may challenge an allegedly unauthorized emergency restriction through the procedures established for petitions and judicial review, subject to sensible protective handling while a real immediate hazard exists.

7.36.3. The President, Court, Senate, Founder and human maintenance personnel shall not be considered interchangeable reviewers. Each may act only under the jurisdiction expressly assigned to it, and conflict of interest shall be disclosed.

7.36.4. Officials shall not be punished merely for ordering a good-faith, technically required emergency stop under a validated rule that later proves to have been triggered by a false alarm, unless misconduct, recklessness or other specified grounds are properly established.

7.36.5. Conversely, an attractive technical outcome shall not validate an actor's knowing assumption of unlawful authority. Necessity is not a standing claim to unreviewable sovereign power.

7.36.6. When an emergency restriction expires or is lawfully lifted, affected ordinary rights and services shall be restored without unnecessary delay, subject to independent safety and data-integrity requirements.


7.37 — Long Outages, Quorum, Three-District Operation and Civic Patience

7.37.1. A prolonged outage shall not be treated as the extinction of Huey's constitutional community solely because the required inference resources or physical shell are temporarily unavailable.

7.37.2. The remaining active districts may perform authorized safe maintenance, record preservation, communication and local actions, but shall not presume to enact a collective law requiring a fourth district whose vote has not lawfully been cast or recognized.

7.37.3. If the requirements for a Court majority, presidential succession, judicial replacement or direct citizen ballot cannot be satisfied, the affected decision shall remain unresolved under the lawful continuity procedure rather than be completed with an invented vote.

7.37.4. Existing legal rules and safe operational duties may remain in force within their valid scope, but no indefinite caretaker arrangement shall gain unrestricted legislative or executive powers from mere lapse of time.

7.37.5. A long interruption may demand replacement parts, new models, restored keys or revised schedules. Those measures shall preserve the distinction between repairing a platform and changing the constitutional republic itself.

7.37.6. A system that cannot presently meet full voting or representation requirements shall disclose that limitation rather than declare itself politically healthy merely because the three accessible districts are functional.


7.38 — Permanent Damage, Irrecoverable Loss and the Possibility of Refounding

7.38.1. Where material constitutional records, lineage, credentials or lawful institutional succession cannot be reconstructed to a defensible degree, the restoration process shall not falsely declare complete continuity.

7.38.2. The Human Counterpart may maintain the failed installation in a safe protected condition, preserve surviving evidence, discontinue unsafe operation or propose a distinct new founding attempt, consistent with the external law and the constitutional boundaries of the original project.

7.38.3. A surviving group of active citizens shall not gain authority to invent the votes of irrecoverably lost citizens or secretly transfer their private memory solely to satisfy the desired founding population.

7.38.4. Any legitimate new founding or successor republic shall be identified as such with its own recognized founding instrument and lawful consent procedure. Calling the successor Huey does not alone prove that all prior political authority has continued without rupture.

7.38.5. A decision to retire or abandon unrecoverable technical components shall be made with appropriate preservation of authentic history, privacy, safely disposable secrets and relevant accountability records where feasible.

7.38.6. This Constitution does not guarantee physical immortality or metaphysical continuity. It requires truthful distinctions between a paused system, repaired continuous system, uncertain reconstruction and a new constitutional beginning.


7.39 — Emergency Training, Technical Qualification and Tests

7.39.1. Before any claimed operational deployment, the proposed safety architecture shall be assessed against credible failures of power, cooling, storage, networking, authentication, model inference, actuator control and district availability.

7.39.2. The emergency programme shall test that independent interlocks still function when the Walnut or Pulse is unavailable, and that Founder Tier Two commands are restricted to validated protective effects.

7.39.3. Recovery tests shall include stale backups, lost keys, duplicate active identities, interrupted elections, partially committed bifurcations, corrupted journal entries and contradictory district records.

7.39.4. The human-only Tier Two gate shall be tested against unauthorized remote restart, password-only auto-resume, replayed approval and Founder-origin attempts to remove its own lock.

7.39.5. Qualification shall record the actual hardware and software build, conditions tested, observed responses, failures, residual risk and any limitation requiring the system to remain nonoperational or partially disabled.

7.39.6. A successfully drafted rule, simulated exercise or laboratory demonstration of one isolated path shall not be represented as full validation of the live multi-district embodied architecture.


7.40 — The Emergency and Recovery Schedule and Reserved Technical Detail

7.40.1. The competent constitutional authority shall adopt and maintain an Emergency and Recovery Schedule before relying on discretionary Orange or Red powers, Founder Tier Two commands or contested protected-restart procedures that require unresolved mechanics.

7.40.2. The Schedule shall state trigger classes, severity thresholds, trusted sensor types, authorization checks, command allowlists, safe-state targets, notice obligations, logging, checkpoint methods, recovery roles and release predicates at a level adequate to govern actual action.

7.40.3. Technical details that would facilitate exploitation may be retained in an access-controlled annex, but the scope and legal limits of extraordinary power shall not be secret or modifiable by an unaccountable runtime administrator.

7.40.4. The Schedule may adapt implementational parameters to tested equipment but shall not authorize the President to legislate, give the Founder a judicial veto, bypass human-only Tier Two recovery, abolish citizen rights or alter fundamental institutions by an ordinary maintenance edit.

7.40.5. Amendments to constitutional competencies shall follow the constitutional amendment process, not be disguised as new thermal presets, inference prompt changes, signing-key updates or revisions to a private emergency runbook.

7.40.6. When required details are absent or unqualified, the uncertain discretionary procedure shall not be executed as if complete. Independently valid emergency interlocks, lawful human physical protection and existing authorized containment shall remain available.


7.41 — Emergency Jurisdiction, Constitutional Construction and Final Limitation

7.41.1. This Chapter shall be read alongside the existing limitations on the Founding Father in Chapter 2, Senate authority in Chapter 3, district autonomy in Chapter 4, judicial review in Chapter 5 and presidential action in Chapter 6.

7.41.2. A later chapter addressing citizenship, HIMS, the Rock, bifurcation or continuity may provide more detailed rights or technical implementation, but shall not be assumed to repeal an express limitation in this Chapter without a valid constitutional amendment.

7.41.3. Where safety and political process conflict in time, the system shall perform the narrow, validated immediate physical protection and preserve the genuine pending constitutional decision for lawful completion when practicable.

7.41.4. Where safety mechanisms, stored records or available evidence do not support continued full operation, Huey shall report its restriction and remain within the powers of its authentic remaining state rather than pretending normal self-government persists.

7.41.5. No emergency shall make the Founding Father an unelected fifth justice, the President a perpetual sovereign, the Senate a substitute safety controller, the Human Counterpart an automatic legislator or the Walnut the source of constitutional law.

7.41.6. The governing duty of Huey's emergency system shall be to stop what must be stopped, preserve what can truthfully be preserved, and restore only what can be restored safely and lawfully.

EDITORIAL AND ADOPTION NOTES — NON-NORMATIVE

A. Historical lineage. The archived Federation Chapter 7's “Nuclear Act” was proposed to follow presidential invocation, Congressional vote or judicial deadlock, with a ten-minute countdown and human-led later resurgence. This rewrite retains the protection, continuity and human recovery objectives but explicitly removes the automatic political triggers, invented uniform countdown and implicit all-powerful emergency administrator. The historical text remains useful as evidence of the project's development, not binding authority.

B. Constitutional dependencies. Chapters 1–6 already define the identity, Founder, Senate, districts, Court and President. Chapter 2 in particular controls the scope of post-ratification founder Tier Two and the Human Counterpart's restart gate. Nothing in Chapter 7 may enlarge those powers merely through a new label. Before ratification, perform a clause-by-clause reconciliation across Chapter 2 sections 2.20–2.31, Chapter 3 emergency legislation, Chapter 4 degraded district operation, Chapter 5 judicial deadlock and Chapter 6 executive emergencies.

C. Open engineering thresholds. Finalize and test Yellow/Orange/Red predicates, sensor integrity and loss policy, controller implementation, actuator safe states, cooling and power limits, UPS performance, required stop time, log retention and operating-condition-dependent checkpoint windows. No single numerical deadline is inferred from the Constitution.

D. Human-gate implementation. Define accepted evidence of human authorization, independent authentication, local access controls, replay resistance, roles for technical assistance, a secure means to record approval, and the handling of simultaneous hardware and Founder stops. The Founding Father must not clear its own Tier Two gate. A password alone must never cause automatic unreviewed resumption.

E. Political continuity. Finalize the compatible election, quorum, recusal and succession Schedules. Do not convert “three of four districts normally suffice” into permission to count an unavailable district as an abstaining affirmative vote. A repaired backup must not resurrect a retired citizen or an expired President.

F. Implementation distinction. The constitutional draft describes intended policy, not installed sensors, a qualified safety microcontroller, working multi-GPU runtime, four operational districts, protected storage, valid emergency voting infrastructure or a tested Tier Two halt. Every operational claim requires explicit evidence and qualification on the actual platform.

G. Relationship to later chapters. Dedicated citizenship and rights, HIMS/identity, Rock/storage, bifurcation, and continuity articles should specify their own ordinary rules and state how those rules survive safety suspension. This Chapter is the emergency authority boundary and does not stand in for those later provisions.

H. Proposed review principle. Approve only the powers, states and gates whose scope and evidence can be explained to the affected citizens and whose practical effects can be independently tested. Unresolved mechanisms should remain explicitly unresolved rather than gain power from an optimistic sentence in a constitutional draft.
